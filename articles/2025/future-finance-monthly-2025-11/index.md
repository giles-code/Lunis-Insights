## Executive Summary

The international regulatory environment is characterised by a decisive shift from framework design to implementation and supervision, particularly in the European Union. Concurrently, the United States is navigating a significant recalibration of its regulatory approach to digital assets, driven by judicial review and legislative initiatives. The United Kingdom is advancing its bespoke agenda focused on stablecoins and market modernisation, while APAC jurisdictions are refining their oversight of Virtual Asset Service Providers (VASPs). Global standard-setting bodies continue to drive convergence on tokenisation, financial crime, and systemic stability.

#### European Union: Operationalising DORA and MiCA

The EU is intensively focused on implementing and integrating its comprehensive digital finance architecture.

- **Operational Resilience (DORA):** Implementation of the Digital Operational Resilience Act is accelerating. The European Supervisory Authorities (ESAs) have designated the initial cohort of Critical ICT Third-Party Providers (CTPPs) and initiated direct oversight.<sup>3</sup> The ECB has issued the TIBER-EU implementation guide for significant institutions under the Single Supervisory Mechanism (SSM), clarifying expectations for threat-led penetration testing.<sup>4</sup> Furthermore, the DORA Reporting Framework (DPM 2.0) establishes the technical standards for incident reporting.

- **Digital Assets (MiCA):** The transition to MiCA is underway, with ESMA issuing critical Q&As clarifying responsibilities for legacy Title II tokens (pre-Dec 2024) and the distinctions between Execution, RTO, and Exchange services.<sup>5</sup> Authorities issued a statement supporting the smooth implementation of data standards and formats, while simultaneously warning consumers of inherent risks and limited protections.

- **Regulatory Architecture and AI:** The proposed “Market Integration and Supervision” (MIS) Package and the EU Digital Omnibus Package signal forthcoming adjustments to the supervisory landscape. The EBA has also begun mapping the AI Act’s requirements to DORA and crypto regulations, indicating an integrated approach to the governance of emerging technologies.

- **Financial Crime:** Supervisory vigilance remains high, demonstrated by the Central Bank of Ireland’s enforcement action against Coinbase Europe Limited regarding AML/CFT compliance.

#### United Kingdom: Stability and Market Modernisation

The UK is refining its domestic frameworks, prioritising the regulation of systemic digital assets and enhancing financial infrastructure

- **Digital Assets:** The Bank of England proposed a regulatory regime for sterling-denominated systemic stablecoins. The FCA launched a dedicated stablecoin cohort within its Regulatory Sandbox. However, the Consumer Panel issued a warning regarding the risks associated with fund tokenisation.

- **Infrastructure and Payments:** The UK successfully transitioned to T+1 settlement, enhancing market efficiency. A new strategy for Future Retail Payments Infrastructure has also been outlined.

- **Financial Crime:** Compliance expectations are intensifying. The Serious Fraud Office (SFO) updated its guidance on evaluating corporate compliance programmes, the NCA UKFIU published SARs best practice guidance, and an FCA multi-firm review identified material weaknesses in financial crime systems and controls.

- **Regulatory Cooperation:** Efforts are underway to boost collaboration between the UK and US financial systems.

#### United States: Regulatory Recalibration and Legislative Efforts

The US regulatory approach to digital assets is undergoing a notable shift, moving away from an enforcement-centric posture towards structured rulemaking and legislative engagement.

- **Regulatory Strategy:** The “Regulation by Enforcement” era is facing substantial challenges, evidenced by strategic dismissals of ongoing cases and the judicial vacatur of the SEC Dealer Rule. The SEC is consolidating its approach under “Project Crypto” and has outlined its 2026 Priorities for the Division of Examinations.

- **Market Structure and Prudential Guidance:** The bipartisan Market Structure Discussion Draft (Boozman, Booker) represents a significant step toward a comprehensive federal framework. Prudential regulators are clarifying their stances; the OCC issued Interpretive Letter 1186, and the FDIC rescinded previously restrictive crypto guidance.

- **Policy and Taxation:** Key developments include the repeal of the CFPB Digital Wallet Rule, ongoing discussions regarding the monetary policy implications of a “Global Stablecoin Glut,” and the provision of tax clarity via the IRS Staking Safe Harbour (Rev. Proc. 2025-31).

#### Asia-Pacific and Canada

- **🇦🇺 Australia:** The government has announced new digital asset laws intended to create a comprehensive framework that balances innovation with investor protection.

- **🇭🇰 Hong Kong:** The SFC is refining its VASP regime, issuing circulars focused on AML/CFT controls (specifically targeting layering activities), and providing guidance on shared liquidity and the expansion of product offerings by virtual asset trading platforms.

- **🇨🇦 Canada:** The Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) has revised the capital and liquidity treatment of crypto asset exposures for banks and insurers, adjusting prudential requirements.

#### International Standards

Global bodies continue to coordinate policy, prioritise future work, and address the implications of emerging technologies.

- **FSB:** The Financial Stability Board Plenary approved its 2026 Work Plan, indicating future priorities for global financial stability.

- **FATF:** The Financial Action Task Force published new guidance on asset recovery, specifically focusing on the challenges posed by virtual assets.

- **IOSCO:** The International Organisation of Securities Commissions issued its final report on the tokenisation of financial assets, providing recommendations for securities regulators.

## Europe

### Reg:Architecture: The Proposed “Market Integration and Supervision” (MIS) Package

The European Commission is proposing the “MIS Package” to centralise financial market supervision under the European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA). This involves transferring direct supervision of “Significant” entities—including CCPs, CSDs, Trading Venues, and CASPs—from national regulators to ESMA. The goal is to reduce cross-border compliance costs and regulatory arbitrage. The package also significantly expands the DLT Pilot Regime threshold to €100 billion.

**CASP Firm Impacts**

- **Centralised Supervision:** ESMA will become the direct supervisor for “Significant” CASPs, shifting the primary regulatory relationship away from local National Competent Authorities (NCAs).

- **Fees:** CASPs transferred to ESMA supervision will pay supervisory fees directly to ESMA, proportionate to turnover/size.

- **Enforcement:** CASPs should expect a more uniform, potentially stricter enforcement culture, with ESMA empowered to impose directly enforceable fines across the EU.

**Following Steps Timeline - Europe**

- **Current Status:** Working Draft/Sensitive Proposal.

- **2025:** Formal adoption of the proposal by the Commission is expected.

- **12–24 Months Post-Adoption:** European Parliament and Council review and negotiation (Co-decision process).

- **Entry into Force:** 20 days after publication in the Official Journal (following agreement).

### RegArchitecture: EU Digital Omnibus Package

The European Commission has proposed a “Digital Omnibus” featuring a Single European Reporting Portal.1 This portal allows firms to submit a single cyber incident report that simultaneously satisfies the obligations under DORA (Digital Operational Resilience Act), NIS2 (Network & Information Systems), and GDPR.2

**CASP Firm Impacts**

- **Efficiency:** Reduction in administrative overhead and the “crisis tax” associated with duplicating incident reports during an active cyberattack.

- **Taxonomy Alignment:** CASPs must map internal definitions of incidents (e.g., DORA major incident vs. GDPR data breach) to ensure that a single report triggers the correct fields across all regimes, avoiding accidental admissions of violations.

- **Technical Integration:** Incident Response platforms (e.g., ServiceNow, Jira) must be reconfigured to push data via API to the new EU gateway.

**Following Steps Timeline - Europe**

- **18 Nov 2025:** Proposal Published (Completed).

- **Q1-Q2 2026:** Parliament & Council Review/Amendments.

- **Late 2026:** Final Adoption of the Regulation (Projected).

- **2027:** Go-Live of Single Portal (Projected).

[🔗 Link](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/digital-omnibus-regulation-proposal)

### DORA: ECB TIBER-EU implementation guide for significant institutions under SSM

The ECB adopted the TIBER-EU framework to operationalise the mandatory Threat-Led Penetration Testing (TLPT) required under DORA for Significant Institutions (SIs) under the SSM. It standardises testing against advanced cyber threats at least every 3 years, mandating the use of external providers and strict confidentiality.

**CASP Firm Impacts**

- **Indirect Impact:** This specific guide applies to SIs under the SSM (primarily large banks). However, it sets the standard for DORA TLPT implementation.

- **Operational Standards:** CASPs required by their regulators to perform TLPT under DORA will likely adhere to these principles, including the mandatory use of external Threat Intelligence Providers (TIPs) and Red Team Testers (RTTs), strict secrecy (with the Blue Team unaware), and robust risk assessments for live production systems.

Following Steps Timeline - Europe

The TIBER-EU framework is operational and structured in three phases

- **Phase I (Preparation):** Entity identification, Control Team setup, scoping, and procurement.

- **Phase II (Testing):** Threat Intelligence gathering, Red Team Test Plan development, and active testing.

- **Phase III (Closure):** Reporting, finalisation of the remediation plan, and official Attestation.

- **Frequency:** Testing is required at least every three years.

[🔗 Link](https://www.bankingsupervision.europa.eu/ecb/pub/pdf/ssm.supervisory_guide202511.en.pdf)

### DORA: ESAs Designate Critical ICT Third-Party Providers (CTPPs) under DORA

On November 18, 2025, the ESAs published the list of designated Critical ICT Third-Party Providers (CTPPs) under DORA (including major cloud providers). This formalises direct ESA oversight of CTPPs and triggers heightened compliance requirements for financial entities that use them.

**CASP Firm Impacts**

- **High Priority:** MiCA-authorised CASPs are in scope for DORA. Their heavy reliance on cloud providers (now CTPPs) necessitates a high-priority operational review.

- **Concentration Risk:** Regulators will closely scrutinise CASPs’ dependencies, concentration risks, and exit strategies related to these CTPPs.

- **Contractual Burden:** CASPs must ensure contracts with CTPPs meet the strict requirements of DORA Articles 28 and 30 (e.g., audit rights, exit strategies), necessitating reviews and renegotiations.

- **Exit Planning:** CASPs must have detailed, tested, and “exam-ready” plans to migrate critical services away from a CTPP if necessary.

**Following Steps Timeline - Europe**

- **Nov 18, 2025:** CTPP list published (Trigger Date).

- **Q4 2025:** Firms must cross-reference vendor registers and start DORA-specific contractual negotiations with CTPPs.

- **Jan 17, 2026:** DORA Applicability Date (Initial Compliance).

- **FY 2026:** CTPPs enter direct oversight; CASPs will receive new compliance requests from CTPPs aligning with ESA requirements.

- **Penalties:** Failure to report in the standardised format will result in regulatory fines.

- **Implementation Costs:** Firms should anticipate technical costs associated with the necessary XBRL mapping.

**Following Steps Timeline - Europe**

- **25 Nov 2025:** Final Technical Package Published (DPM 2.0).

- **Immediate:** Integrate DPM 4.2 standards into production environments and verify software compliance.

- **Q1 2026:** First Mandatory Reports Due (Covering 2025 data).

### MiCA: EU Supervisory Authorities warn consumers of risks and limited protection

The ESAs issued a formal warning to consumers regarding the risks of crypto-assets, emphasising that MiCA protection is limited and does not cover all products.12 They urged consumers to verify provider authorisation via the ESMA central register, especially during the transitional period, and warned against “finfluencer” promotions.

**CASP Firm Impacts**

- **Authorisation as Trust Signal:** The ESMA central register will be the single source of truth. Authorised CASPs gain a trust advantage; unauthorised firms will operate illegally after the transition.

- **Transitional Period Clarity:** CASPs operating under the transitional period must clearly communicate that their clients do not benefit from MiCA protections until the provider is fully authorised.

- **Marketing Scrutiny:** Increased regulatory attention on aggressive promotion and social media marketing.

**Following Steps Timeline - Europe**

- **December 30, 2024:** MiCA rules for most CASPs apply; ESMA central register goes live.

- **July 1, 2026:** Transitional period ends. All CASPs operating in the EU must be fully authorised under MiCA.

### MiCA: ESMA Q&A on White Paper responsibilities for legacy Title II tokens (pre-Dec 2024)

ESMA clarified the responsibilities for white papers of “legacy” crypto-assets traded before 30 December 2024. Offerors/Issuers are exempt from creating them, but they must still comply with marketing rules. The responsibility shifts entirely to Operators of Trading Platforms, who have until 31 December 2027 to ensure compliant white papers are published for any legacy tokens they list.

**CASP Firm Impacts**

- **Trading Platforms (High Burden):** Face a significant compliance task. They must ensure that white papers are available by the deadline, either producing them or requiring issuers to provide them for continued listing.

- **Other CASPs (e.g., Custodians) (Low Burden):** Only required to publish hyperlinks to existing white papers; not responsible for their creation.

- **Offerors/Issuers (Relief):** Exempt from the white paper requirement for legacy tokens but must comply with MiCA marketing rules from 30 Dec 2024.

**Following Steps Timeline - Europe**

- **30 December 2024:** MiCA’s marketing rules apply to new marketing communications for these legacy tokens.

- **31 December 2027:** Deadline for trading platforms to ensure compliant white papers are published for listed legacy Title II tokens.

[🔗 Link](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-data/questions-answers/2654)

### MiCA: ESMA Q&A on distinguishing between Execution, RTO, and Exchange services

ESMA clarified how NCAs must distinguish between Execution, RTO, and Exchange services. NCAs must analyse the “operational reality,” not the CASP’s label. The key is the “Reasonable Client” Test: if a client expects the “best possible result” (best execution), the service is Execution, even if the firm is the counterparty. If in doubt, especially for retail clients, NCAs should presume “Execution.”

**CASP Firm Impacts**

- **Risk of Re-classification:** Regulators will classify services based on operational reality during MiCA licensing, regardless of the CASP’s description.

- **Best Execution Burden:** Classification as “Execution” imposes the significant operational burden of providing, monitoring, and proving best execution.

- **Ambiguity Liability:** Unclear client agreements or marketing will likely lead to the stricter “Execution” classification.

- **‘Exchange’ Model Clarity:** Operating as an “Exchange” (to avoid best execution) requires unambiguous clarity that the firm is only a counterparty offering a firm price.

**Following Steps Timeline - Europe**

- **Immediate:** The guidance is practical immediately for NCAs assessing MiCA applications. CASP applicants must review their models and documentation against this guidance.

- **Ongoing:** NCAs will apply the “operational reality” test and “presumption of execution” when reviewing license applications.

[🔗 Link](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-data/questions-answers/2653)

### MiCA: Statement to support the smooth implementation of MiCA data standards and format requirements

On 28 November 2025, ESMA released the final technical specifications, schemas, and formats required for CASPs and issuers to comply with MiCA. This update focuses on the technical execution of regulatory requirements, mandating machine-readable formats for order book records, transaction reporting, and white papers. ESMA has specified the required JSON schemas (based on ISO 20022) and XBRL taxonomies for reporting and transparency.

**CASP Firm Impacts**

- **Technical Data Formatting (JSON):** Systems must be upgraded to export Order Book and Record Keeping data in the specified JSON format (per the ISO 20022 methodology). Proprietary formats (e.g., CSV, XML) will likely be rejected by National Competent Authorities (NCAs) after the grace period.

- **White Paper Compliance (iXBRL):** Issuers and platforms must shift from PDF-only white papers to the Inline XBRL (iXBRL) format (XHTML with embedded XBRL tags), requiring specialised tagging software or CMS updates.

- **Mandatory Identifiers (LEI/DTI):** CASPs must ensure all corporate clients have a Legal Entity Identifier (LEI) and all traded crypto-assets have a Digital Token Identifier (DTI). Trading platforms are responsible for requesting a DTI via the DTIF if one is missing for a listed token.

- **Automated Supervision:** The standardised, machine-readable formats enable regulators to perform automated cross-market analysis, making discrepancies between internal records and public data easily detectable.

**Following Steps Timeline - Europe**

- **05 Aug 2025:** XBRL Taxonomy for White Papers published.

- **28 Nov 2025:** Release of JSON schemas for Order Books & Record Keeping.

- **23 Dec 2025:** White Paper ITS application date (iXBRL compliance mandatory).

- **May 2026:** End of Grace Period (6 months post-publication). NCAs will begin strictly enforcing the submission of Order Book data in the new JSON format.

[🔗 Link](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-11/ESMA75-1303207761-6284_Statement_to_support_the_smooth_implementation_of_MiCA_standards_and_format.pdf)

### FinCrime: Central Bank of Ireland enforcement action against Coinbase Europe Limited

The Central Bank of Ireland fined Coinbase Europe Limited €21.46 million for severe AML/CFT breaches between 2021 and 2025.15. A faulty transaction monitoring system resulted in over 30 million transactions (€176 billion) not being monitored—a delayed review led to 2,708 late Suspicious Transaction Reports (STRs) linked to potential serious crimes.

**CASP Firm Impacts**

- **End of Grace Period:** This first enforcement action signals that crypto firms are now subject to the same rigorous enforcement as traditional finance.

- **Increased Scrutiny:** All CASPs/VASPs are on notice. Regulators will actively audit AML/CFT systems, treating crypto as high-risk requiring robust controls.

- **Severe Penalties:** Systemic failures, even unintentional “configuration faults,” will attract severe penalties.

**Following Steps Timeline - Europe**

- **06 November 2025:** Public announcement of the fine.

- **Future (Undated):** The Central Bank will apply to the High Court to formally confirm the sanctions.

- **Ongoing:** The national Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU) will investigate the 2,708 late STRs.

[🔗 Link](https://www.centralbank.ie/news/article/press-release-enforcement-action-against-coinbase-europe-limited-6-November-2025)

### AI: EBA Maps AI Act to DORA and Crypto Regulations

The EBA analysed the interplay between the EU AI Act, DORA, and MiCA. It concluded that DORA’s existing requirements for ICT risk management and data governance already cover many obligations for “high-risk” AI systems under the AI Act, indicating regulatory intent to avoid double-regulation.

**CASP Firm Impacts**

- **Compliance Efficiency:** CASPs utilising AI (e.g., for trading, risk management, AML) can leverage their existing DORA-compliant architectures to address significant portions of the AI Act.

- **Strategic Alignment:** Firms are advised to integrate their AI governance frameworks directly into their DORA ICT risk frameworks for efficiency.

**Following Steps Timeline - Europe**

- **Immediate Action:** Firms should review their AI governance to ensure it aligns with their DORA implementation plans.

**Q1 2026:** The European Commission is expected to issue formal guidelines on the interplay between the AI Act and sectoral legislation.

## United Kingdom

### Digital Assets: BoE: Proposed regulatory regime for sterling-denominated systemic stablecoins

- **Summary:** The Bank of England (BoE) is consulting on a revised regulatory regime for systemic sterling-denominated stablecoins, to be jointly managed with the FCA. The proposal revises the 2023 approach, moving away from 100% central bank deposit backing to a 40/60 split: at least 40% in unremunerated BoE deposits and up to 60% in short-term UK government debt. Temporary holding limits (individuals: £20k; businesses: £10m) are proposed to mitigate financial stability risks during the adoption period.

- **What it means for firms:**

  - **Commercial Viability:** The 40/60 split allows issuers to earn a return. A “Step-Up Regime” supports innovation by allowing firms to hold up to 95% of government debt initially.

  - **Capital Requirements:** Issuers must hold risk-based general business capital (e.g., six months’ operating expenses) and separate reserves in a statutory trust for coinholders.

  - **Operational Requirements:** Non-UK issuers must establish a UK subsidiary. Issuers must guarantee redemption at par by the end of the business day and cannot pay interest to coinholders.

  - **Technology:** The BoE is open to permissionless public ledgers, but the issuer remains fully accountable for all associated risks (e.g., settlement finality and resilience).

- **Timeline:**

  - **10 February 2026:** Consultation Closes.

  - **2026:** BoE to consult on and finalise the Codes of Practice (final rules); BoE and FCA to consult on the detailed design of the joint regulatory framework; BoE to publish its specific supervisory approach.

[🔗 Link](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/paper/2025/cp/proposed-regulatory-regime-for-sterling-denominated-systemic-stablecoins)

### Digital Assets: FCA launches stablecoin cohort of Regulatory Sandbox

- **Summary:** The FCA has opened a specific Regulatory Sandbox cohort for prospective UK stablecoin issuers, active as of 26 November 2025. The objective is to allow firms to test stablecoin issuance models (retail or wholesale) in a controlled environment while helping the regulator refine the emerging rules (CP25/14 and CP25/25). Applicants must demonstrate alignment with proposed policies on redemption, asset backing, and custody.

- **What it means for firms:**

  - **“First Mover” vs. “Guinea Pig” Trade-off:** Participation offers a “safe harbour” advantage before the whole regime is finalised, but requires exposing proprietary mechanics and data to deep regulatory scrutiny.

  - **Pre-requisite of “Regulatory Maturity”:** Firms likely need a robust compliance infrastructure (e.g., AML registration, PSR permissions) for live testing. Pure tech startups may be limited to testing with “firm data.”

  - **Alignment with CP25/14 is Mandatory:** The “Test Plan” must explicitly map to the proposals in CP25/14, particularly regarding redemption rights and backing assets.

  - **Reputation and Signalling:** Successful acceptance provides significant market legitimacy and acts as a trust signal to partners and investors.

- **Timeline (Application Window):**

  - **Phase 1 (28 Nov – 5 Dec 2025):** Feasibility & Gap Analysis (Review form, CP25/14 gap analysis, permission check).

  - **Phase 2 (8 Dec – 22 Dec 2025):** Drafting the Test Plan (Define metrics, allocate resources, draft narrative).

  - **Phase 3 (5 Jan – 12 Jan 2026):** Internal Review & Refinement (Compliance and stakeholder sign-off).

  - **Phase 4 (14 Jan – 16 Jan 2026):** Submission (recommended).

  - **18 January 2026 (11:59 pm):** Deadline.

[🔗 Link](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/innovation/regulatory-sandbox/stablecoins-cohort)

### Digital Assets: Consumer Panel Issues Warning on Fund Tokenisation Risks

- **Summary:** The Financial Services Consumer Panel responded to the FCA’s consultation on Fund Tokenisation (CP25/28), warning that tokenised funds must carry rights and protections equivalent to traditional funds. The Panel highlighted concerns regarding Operational Resilience (reliance on cloud infrastructure) and Governance Risks (complex conflicts of interest in DLT models).

- **What it means for firms:**

  - **High Barriers for D2C:** Increased scrutiny and high barriers to entry are expected for “Direct-to-Consumer” (D2C) tokenised funds, with rigid custody and disclosure requirements.

  - **Mandatory Disclosures:** Likely requirement for “health warnings” in retail disclosures flagging the experimental nature of the technology and specific DLT risks.

- **Timeline:**

  - **Current:** FCA is reviewing feedback from the consultation.

  - **Q1/Q2 2026:** Policy Statement (final rules) is expected to be published.

### RegArchitecture: CP25/33: Fees and Levies

- **Summary:** The FCA’s Consultation Paper CP25/33 proposes a fee structure for the 2026/27 financial year, aiming to create a low barrier to entry for the crypto sector. It proposes a flat annual fee of £2,200 for all authorised cryptoasset firms. A variable fee applies only to firms with regulated income exceeding £500,000.

- **What it means for firms:**

  - **Low Barrier to Entry:** The £2,200 baseline removes a significant financial hurdle for startups seeking authorisation.

  - **Variable Costs for Scale:** Firms exceeding the £500k revenue threshold will face variable fees (rate to be consulted on in Spring 2026).

  - **Revenue Classification:** Firms must accurately define and separate “regulated income” from other revenue streams to manage fee liability.

  - **Increased Competition:** The low entry fee is expected to increase the number of authorised competitors.

- **Timeline:**

  - **Jan 16, 2026:** Consultation Closes.

  - **March 2026:** FCA publishes final rules on fee structure.

  - **April 2026:** FCA consults on the actual variable fee rates.

  - **April 1, 2026:** New fee structure takes effect for the 2026/27 financial year.

  - **July 2026:** Invoices are typically issued.

[🔗 Link](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/consultation-papers/cp25-33-regulatory-fees-and-levies-policy-proposals-2026-27)

### Payments: Strategy for Future Retail Payments Infrastructure

- **Summary:** The Payments Vision Delivery Committee (HMT, BoE, FCA, PSR) outlined the strategy for the UK’s future retail payments infrastructure, aiming to build a system based on Innovation, Competition, and Security. Key outcomes include enabling account-to-account (A2A) payments at the point of sale and ensuring seamless operation in a “multi-money” ecosystem (e.g., stablecoins, tokenised deposits, potential digital pound).

- **What it means for firms:**

  - **“Multi-Money” Interoperability Mandate:** The new infrastructure must be built to facilitate interoperability across emerging forms of digital money, ensuring CASPs are integrated into core UK payment systems.

  - **Fair Access:** The strategy mandates fair, transparent, and non-discriminatory access to the infrastructure for a wide range of Payment Service Providers

- **Timeline:**

  - **Starting Nov 2025:** The new Retail Payments Infrastructure Board (RPIB) begins designing the new infrastructure; the industry begins establishing the new, industry-owned Delivery Company.

  - **Delivery:** Described as a “multi-year endeavour” with no specific end date.

### Payments: UK’s move to T+1 Settlement

- **Summary:** The UK Government published a draft Statutory Instrument mandating a shift to a T+1 settlement cycle (settling trades one business day after execution) by October 11, 2027.This aligns the UK with the US and aims to modernise infrastructure, reduce systemic risk, and improve capital efficiency.

- **What it means for firms:**

  - **Reduced Funding Drag:** Moving capital from traditional assets (T+1) to crypto markets (T+0) becomes faster, improving capital efficiency for cross-asset strategies.

  - **Faster On/Off-Ramps:** Banks must speed up fiat transactions to meet T+1 requirements, thereby accelerating the interface between the banking system and crypto exchanges.

  - **Reduced Counterparty Risk:** A shorter settlement window reduces the risk of counterparty default for institutional crypto firms working with traditional brokers.

- **Timeline:**

  - **Feb 27, 2026:** Consultation Deadline on the draft Statutory Instrument.

  - **Dec 31, 2026:** Recommended industry deadline for “pre-settlement” operational readiness.

  - **Q1-Q3 2027:** Industry-wide End-to-End Testing.

The focus is on ensuring uniform, stricter global implementation of rules to close gaps, prevent regulatory arbitrage, and address risks associated with Non-Bank Financial Intermediation (NBFI).

- **CASP Implications (Specifically Stablecoin Issuers):**

  - **Pressure on Arbitrage Hubs:** Firms operating in jurisdictions with lighter regulation will face significant pressure as the FSB pushes for global consistency.

  - **Increased Compliance Costs:** Multi-jurisdictional stablecoin issuers should anticipate higher compliance costs.

  - **Stricter Prudential Requirements:** Expect tighter rules on reserve quality, liquidity, and capital requirements amid heightened scrutiny of "interlinkage” risks (e.g., run risks, redemption gates).

  - **New Standards:** New standards are anticipated for stablecoin reserve quality and liquidity, as well as for mandatory NBFI disclosures.

- **Following Steps Timeline - International:**

  - **Nov 19, 2025 (Completed):** FSB Plenary Approval of the 2026 Work Plan.

  - **Short-Term:** Stakeholders (e.g., Global Policy Heads) must review the 2026 roadmap and its expected deliverables.

  - **2026:** The FSB will drive efforts to close “gaps and inconsistencies” globally. Expect the release of consultation papers and final standards on reserves and NBFI disclosures.

### FinCrime: FATF Publishes New Guidance on Asset Recovery, Targeting Virtual Assets

- **Summary:** On November 4, 2025, the Financial Action Task Force (FATF) published “Asset Recovery Guidance and Best Practices.” The guidance stresses that national authorities and Law Enforcement Agencies (LEAs) must develop the operational capabilities to trace, seize, and confiscate virtual assets (VAs). It notes that traditional methods are insufficient and that rapid action is required, given the speed and global reach of VA networks.

- **What it means for firms:**

  - **Sophisticated LEA Requests:** CASPs can expect increased pressure and more sophisticated requests from LEAs trained in blockchain analysis and on-chain seizure techniques.

  - **Rapid Response Required:** Firms must be prepared to act quickly on seizure requests, as the FATF highlights that the “window of opportunity for seizure may close, often within minutes.”

  - **Reinforced AML/CFT Obligations:** The guidance reinforces the critical importance of existing AML/CFT compliance (under Recommendation 15). Data collected by CASPs, such as “Travel Rule” information, is essential to the FATF's asset recovery efforts.

- **Following Steps Timeline - International:**

  - **2023 (Completed):** FATF revised Recommendations on asset recovery.

  - **Nov 4, 2025 (Completed):** Publication of the new Asset Recovery Guidance.

  - **Ongoing:** National jurisdictions are expected to implement the guidance, and LEAs globally are expected to enhance their “operational awareness” and technical capabilities for virtual asset recovery and confiscation.

### Digital Assets: IOSCO final report on tokenisation of financial assets

- **Summary:** The IOSCO report (FR/17/25) finds that the adoption of financial asset tokenisation (primarily bonds and MMFs) is “incipient and incremental,” not revolutionary. Scalability is currently hindered by two main structural challenges: a lack of interoperability across blockchains (leading to fragmentation) and the absence of high-quality, on-chain settlement assets (e.g., wholesale CBDCs). The report concludes that most risks are existing legal, operational, and technological risks that are manifesting in new ways due to DLT.

- **CASP Implications (Broader Digital Asset Firms):**

  - **Legal and Ownership Ambiguity:** Firms face significant uncertainty regarding the legal status of tokens, the point of finality for settlement (especially on public or L2 networks), and conflicts over the definitive “source of truth” (on-chain ledger vs. off-chain registrar).

  - **Infrastructure and Operational Risks:** Challenges include market fragmentation from non-interoperable DLTs, smart contract vulnerabilities, unique custody risks (loss of private keys), and balancing data privacy (e.g., GDPR) with transparency/AML requirements.

  - **Systemic Risks and Interlinkages:** IOSCO noted “early signs” of spillover risks, such as tokenised MMFs being used as stablecoin reserves and crypto volatility being linked to traditional markets. Reliance on stablecoins for settlement in the absence ofg wCBDC) introduces counterparty and liquidity risks. DLT may also enable the “collapsing” of market functions into single entities, creating concentration risk.

  - **Regulatory Approach:** IOSCO advocates a technology-neutral approach (“same activity, same risk, same regulatory outcomes”), applying existing securities frameworks rather than creating new rulebooks.

- **Following Steps Timeline - International:**

  - **Current Phase (Incipient):** The market is experimental, focused on pilots and sandboxes. Regulators are applying existing frameworks, issuing specific guidance, and utilising sandbox regimes (e.g., the EU DLT Pilot Regime and the UK DSS).

  - **Medium-Term (Hybrid Model):** DLT and traditional financial infrastructure will co-exist. Interoperability between these systems is the primary operational challenge. Regulators will continue clarifying rules and may apply IOSCO’s Crypto and Digital Asset (CDA) and DeFi Recommendations.

  - **Long-Term (Scaled Adoption):** Widespread adoption is contingent on achieving interoperability and establishing reliable on-chain settlement assets.

[🔗 Link](https://www.iosco.org/news/pdf/IOSCONEWS778.pdf?v=4)

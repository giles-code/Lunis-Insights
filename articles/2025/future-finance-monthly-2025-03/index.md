## Executive Summary

This summary highlights key regulatory developments in Europe, primarily focusing on the Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation and the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) and their potential impact on firms' business models.

### Key Regulatory Developments:

- **Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA):** A significant wave of implementing and delegated regulations under MiCA has been published, specifying detailed technical standards and procedures. These cover critical areas such as:

  - Authorisation processes for Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASPs), including application forms, required information, and assessment of qualifying holdings.

  - Technical standards for crypto-asset white papers, ensuring data classification and machine-readability.

  - Operational requirements for issuers of Asset-Referenced Tokens (ARTs) and e-money tokens, including procedures for adjusting funds, governance of remuneration policies, and own funds requirements.

  - Transparency and order book record-keeping for CASPs operating trading platforms.

  - Guidance from ESMA on suitability requirements, periodic statements for portfolio management, and the classification of crypto-assets.

  - Ongoing discussions and consultations include the EBA's public hearing on the draft RTS for crypto exposure calculation.

- **Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA):** The European Commission is actively pursuing the implementation of DORA, evidenced by formal notice letters to member states for non-transposition and the adoption of RTS on sub-contracting ICT services. Further developments include the publication of a Delegated Regulation on joint examination teams and ESA guidelines on estimating the costs of ICT incidents.

- **Financial Crime:** The EBA is consulting on RTS under the AMLA Regulation, AML Regulation, and MLD6, indicating a continued focus on strengthening anti-money laundering frameworks.

- **Digital Markets:** The ECB and EBA's ongoing analysis and publications on crypto lending, borrowing, staking, and broader crypto-asset market trends highlight the regulatory interest in understanding and potentially further regulating these activities.

### Key Impacts on Firms’ Business Models

- **Increased Compliance Burden:** The detailed requirements under MiCA and DORA will necessitate significant investments in compliance frameworks, technology, and personnel for firms operating with crypto assets or within the broader financial sector in Europe.

- **Enhanced Authorisation Processes:** CASPs must adapt their application processes and provide extensive information to meet the new authorisation standards under MiCA.

- **Stricter Operational Requirements:** Issuers of ARTs and e-money tokens will face more stringent rules regarding capital requirements, governance, and operational resilience, potentially impacting their profitability and business models.

- **Greater Transparency and Reporting:** CASPs operating trading platforms must implement new systems for presenting transparency data and maintaining order book records.

- **Focus on Digital Operational Resilience:** DORA will require financial entities to strengthen their ICT risk management, incident response capabilities, and third-party risk management, potentially leading to changes in outsourcing strategies and technology investments.

- **Evolving AML/CFT Landscape:** The ongoing developments in anti-money laundering regulations will require firms to update their procedures and controls continuously.

- **Strategic Considerations for Digital Assets:** The increased regulatory scrutiny and detailed rules for crypto assets will force firms to carefully consider their strategies, potentially leading to adjustments in product offerings and target markets.

In conclusion, the European regulatory landscape is transforming significantly, particularly in digital finance. Firms must proactively engage with these developments, assess their potential impact on their business models, and implement necessary changes to ensure compliance and maintain competitiveness. The focus on crypto-assets and digital operational resilience will continue to shape the financial services industry in Europe in the coming years.

### MiCA: Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2025/306 of 31 October 2024 specifies technical standards for applying Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 regarding standard forms and procedures for crypto-asset service provider authorisation applications

This regulation focuses on streamlining the application process for legal persons or other undertakings seeking authorisation as Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASPs) within the EU, as mandated by Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 (MiCA). Here's a summary and the implications:

**Summary:**

- **Designated Contact Points:**

  - Competent authorities within each member state must establish a specific contact point for receiving CASP authorisation applications.

  - These contact details must be publicly available on the authorities' websites.

- **Standardised Application Form:**

  - Applicants must use a standardised form (provided in the regulation's annexe) to submit their authorisation applications.

  - The application must be submitted in a format that allows storage and future information retrieval.

- **Acknowledgement of Receipt:**

  - Competent authorities must provide applicants with an acknowledgement of receipt, including contact details of the staff handling the application.

- **Notification of Changes:**

  - Applicants must promptly notify competent authorities of any changes to the information provided in their application.

  - If updated information is provided, the application's assessment timeline restarts from the date the updated information is received.

  - Crypto asset service providers must notify the competent authority of any changes to the information under which the authorisation was granted.

- **Communication of Decision:**

  - Competent authorities must notify applicants of their authorisation decision (approval or refusal) in paper form, electronically, or both.

- **Information Requirements:**

  - The application form requires detailed information based on delegated regulation 2025/305, including general information, program of operations, prudential requirements, governance, business continuity, AML/CFT, management body details, shareholder information, ICT systems, client asset segregation, complaint handling, custody, trading platform rules, asset exchange rules, execution policy, advice/portfolio management information, and transfer service information.

**What it means for firms:**

- **Increased Efficiency and Clarity:**

  - Establishing designated contact points and standardised application forms will create a more efficient and transparent application process.

  - This will reduce ambiguity and streamline communication between applicants and competent authorities.

- **Enhanced Compliance:**

  - The requirement for accurate and up-to-date information emphasises the importance of compliance and due diligence for CASPs.

  - Applicants must ensure that all information provided is accurate and that any changes are promptly reported.

- **Clear Communication Channels:**

  - The requirement for acknowledgement of receipt and clear communication of decisions will improve transparency and accountability in the authorisation process.

- **Time Sensitivity:**

  - Applicants must know that any application information changes will restart the assessment timeline.

  - This emphasises the need for thorough preparation and accurate information from the outset.

- **Standardisation of Application Process:**

  - A standardised form will create a uniform approach to CASP authorisation across the EU.

- **Increased Regulatory Oversight:**

  - The regulation serves to increase the oversight of crypto asset service providers.

- **Detailed Documentation:**

  - Crypto asset service providers must maintain very detailed documentation.

### MiCA: Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/414 of 18 December 2024 supplements Regulation (EU) 2023/1114, detailing the information required to assess proposed acquisitions of qualifying holdings in crypto-asset service providers

This regulation establishes detailed information requirements for proposed acquirers of qualifying holdings in crypto-asset service providers (CASPs) within the European Union. Here's a summary of its key points and firm implications:

**Summary:**

- **Purpose:**

  - To ensure the prudential assessment of proposed acquisitions of qualifying holdings in CASPs.

  - To maintain the integrity and stability of the crypto-asset market within the EU.

- **Information Requirements:**

  - Proposed acquirers must provide competent authorities with comprehensive personal and financial details.

  - The required information level varies depending on the acquirer's legal status (natural person, legal person, trust, etc.) and the size of the qualifying holding.

  - Emphasis is placed on assessing the acquirer's reputation, financial soundness, and potential conflicts of interest.

  - Detailed financial information is required, including funding sources and proof of those funds' legal origin, especially when dealing with crypto assets.

  - Information regarding any criminal, civil, or administrative proceedings against the acquirer is mandatory.

  - Information about the acquiring entities’ management and beneficial owners is required.

  - Information about the financing of the acquisition, especially regarding crypto assets, is required.

  - Information about the impact of the acquisition on the target entity supervision is required.

- **Data Protection:**

  - The regulation adheres to data minimisation principles and compliance with Regulation (EU) 2016/679 (GDPR).

  - Competent authorities must only collect necessary personal data and retain it for the required duration.

- **Indirect Holdings:**

  - The information required for indirect holdings is proportionate to the level of influence the acquirer will have.

- **Reduced Information:**

  - Reduced information requirements sometimes apply when the acquirer has been previously assessed or is under the competent authority's supervision.

- **Third Country Entities:**

  - Third-world entities must provide extra information so that the competent authority can ensure that the third country's legal framework does not negatively impact the target entity.

**What it means for firms:**

- **Increased Regulatory Burden:**

  - Proposed acquirers face a significant regulatory burden, requiring extensive documentation and disclosure.

  - This could deter some potential acquirers, particularly smaller entities.

- **Enhanced Due Diligence:**

  - CASPs and their competent authorities must conduct thorough due diligence on proposed acquirers.

  - This includes verifying the accuracy and legitimacy of the provided information.

- **Greater Transparency:**

  - The regulation promotes greater transparency in the crypto-asset market, reducing the risk of illicit activities.

- **Impact on M&A Activity:**

  - The stringent requirements could impact the crypto-asset sector’s merger and acquisition (M&A) activity, potentially slowing down transactions.

- **Compliance Costs:**

  - The costs associated with complying with these regulations could be substantial for firms.

- **Third country limitations:**

  - Acquisitions from third-country entities will be more complicated.

- **Reduced burden in some cases:**

  - In cases where the competent authority already has much of the required information, the reporting burden is reduced.

### MiCA: Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/413 of 18 December 2024 supplements Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 by detailing the information needed to assess proposed acquisitions of qualifying holdings in asset-referenced token issuers

This DR outlines the detailed information requirements for a proposed acquirer of a qualifying holding in an issuer of an asset-referenced token, as mandated by the relevant competent authority. Here's a summary and firm implications:

**Summary:**

- **Notification and Accuracy:**

  - Proposed acquirers must submit comprehensive information for prudential assessment at the time of notification.

  - All information must be true, accurate, complete, and up-to-date throughout the assessment process.

  - Any changes to the provided information must be promptly communicated to the competent authority.

- **Data Requirements:**

  - The notification must include detailed data about the proposed acquirer, management, shareholders, and beneficial owners.

  - Personal data is required, but only what is necessary for the assessment, adhering to data minimisation principles (Regulation (EU) 2016/679).

  - Specific information is required depending on the acquirer's legal form (natural person, legal person, trust, AIF, UCITS, sovereign wealth fund).

- **Reputation and Financial Soundness:**

  - Competent authorities will assess the acquirer's reputation, including criminal records, civil and administrative proceedings, and past assessments by other authorities.

  - Financial soundness is evaluated through financial statements, sources of funding, and potential conflicts of interest.

  - Information on the funding sources of the acquisition must be provided, with evidence of the legal origin of the funds, especially regarding crypto-assets.

- **Impact on Supervision:**

  - The proposed acquisition's impact on the competent authority's ability to supervise the target entity is a key consideration.

  - For legal persons, this includes assessing the impact of consolidated supervision.

- **Varying Levels of Information:**

  - The required information level varies based on the size of the qualifying holding and the acquirer's existing relationship with the competent authority.

  - Reduced information requirements sometimes apply when the acquirer has been previously assessed or is under the competent authority's supervision.

- **Indirect holdings:**

  - The amount of information required for indirect holdings is proportional to the acquirer's effective influence over the target entity.

- **Management body changes:**

  - Suppose the acquirer plans to add new members to the target entity management body. In that case, they must provide the same information as if they were applying for authorisation of the target entity.

**What it means for firms:**

- **Increased Regulatory Scrutiny:**

  - Proposed acquirers face rigorous scrutiny of their financial stability, reputation, and potential impact on the target entity.

  - This regulation prevents unsuitable entities from acquiring significant holdings in asset-referenced token issuers.

- **Data Compliance:**

  - Acquirers must comply with strict data protection requirements, ensuring the accuracy and security of personal information.

- **Transparency and Disclosure:**

  - The regulation promotes transparency by requiring detailed disclosures of financial interests, conflicts of interest, and funding sources.

  - Full transparency is required regarding the origins of the funds and any crypto assets involved in the transaction.

- **Complex Acquisition Processes:**

  - The comprehensive information requirements will likely increase the complexity and time required for acquisition processes.

- **Level of holding effects process:**

  - The percentage of the qualifying holding that is being significantly acquired changes the information that must be provided.

- **Third-country entities:**

  - Third-world entities will have to provide extra information so that the competent authority can ensure that the third country's legal framework does not negatively impact the target entity.

- **Reduced burden in some cases:**

  - In cases where the competent authority already has much of the required information, the reporting burden is reduced.

### MiCA: Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/305 of 31 October 2024 supplements Regulation (EU) 2023/1114 by detailing the regulatory technical standards for information required in crypto-asset service provider authorisation applications

The DR outlines the detailed information requirements for applications seeking authorisation to provide crypto-asset services within the EU. These requirements ensure that competent authorities can properly assess applicants' ability to comply with MiCAR regulations.

**Key areas covered:**

- **General Information:** Details about the applicant's identity, legal structure, and contact information.

- **Programme of Operations:** A comprehensive plan detailing the applicant's business strategy, organisational structure, target clients, marketing activities, and resource allocation.

- **Prudential Requirements:** Information on the applicant's financial safeguards, including own funds, insurance policies, and economic forecasts.

- **Governance Arrangements and Internal Control Mechanisms:** Details on the applicant's organisational structure, management body, compliance procedures, record-keeping, and risk management.

- **Business Continuity Plan:** A plan outlining the steps to ensure the continuity and regularity of crypto-asset services in the face of disruptions.

- **Detection and Prevention of Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing:** Information on the applicant's internal controls, policies, and procedures to comply with anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing regulations.

- **Identity and Proof of Good Repute, Knowledge, Skills, and Experience of the Members of the Management Body:** Background information on the individuals managing the applicant, including their professional history and any potential conflicts of interest.

- **Information Relating to Shareholders or Members with Qualifying Holdings:** Details about the applicant's ownership structure and the identity and background of major shareholders.

- **ICT Systems and Related Security Arrangements:** Technical documentation and security protocols for the applicant's IT infrastructure, including cybersecurity measures.

- **Segregation and Safekeeping of Clients’ Crypto-Assets and Funds:** Procedures for ensuring the segregation and protection of client assets.

- **Complaints-Handling Procedures:** Details on how the applicant will handle and resolve client complaints.

- **Custody and Administration Policy:** Specific requirements for applicants intending to provide custody and administration services for crypto-assets.

- **Operating Rules of the Trading Platform and Market Abuse Detection:** Rules and procedures for applicants operating trading platforms, including admission of crypto-assets, order execution, and market abuse prevention.

- **Exchange of Crypto-Assets for Funds or Other Crypto-Assets:** Information on the applicant's commercial policy and pricing mechanisms for exchange services.

- **Execution Policy:** Details on the applicant's policies and procedures for executing orders on behalf of clients.

- **Provision of Advice on Crypto-Assets or Portfolio Management of Crypto-Assets:** Information on the applicant's arrangements for ensuring the competence of staff providing advice or portfolio management services.

- **Transfer Services:** Details on the applicant's arrangements for providing transfer services for crypto-assets.

**Firm Implications**

- **For Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASPs):** This regulation directly impacts any firm seeking to operate as a CASP within the EU. Firms must meticulously prepare their applications, providing all the required information in detail. This may require significant investment in compliance infrastructure, documentation, and personnel.

- **For Legal and Consulting Firms:** There will be increased demand for legal and consulting services to help firms navigate the complexities of MiCAR compliance and the application process.

- **For Regulators:** Competent authorities must develop robust processes for reviewing and assessing applications, ensuring they have the resources and expertise to handle the volume and complexity of the information provided.

### MiCA: Official translations of ESMA guidelines on suitability requirements and format of periodic statements for portfolio management activities

- **Purpose:** The guidelines aim to consistently apply MiCA's suitability requirements and the format of periodic statements for crypto-asset portfolio management. This is to enhance investor protection.

- **Suitability Requirements:**

  - CASPs must provide clients with clear information about the purpose and scope of the suitability assessment.

  - CASPs must have robust arrangements to understand clients, including using clear and comprehensive questionnaires.

  - The extent to which information is collected from clients should be proportional to the services provided and the complexity/risk of the crypto assets.

  - CASPs must take reasonable steps to ensure the reliability of client information and not unduly rely on client self-assessment.

  - Client information should be regularly updated, especially in ongoing relationships.

  - Specific rules are provided for dealing with client information for legal entities or groups of individuals.

  - CASPs must have procedures to understand the characteristics and risks of crypto-assets.

  - CASPs must have policies and procedures to ensure the suitability of crypto-assets or services.

  - CASPs must assess the costs and complexity of equivalent products.

  - CASPs must analyse the costs and benefits of switching investments.

  - Staff involved in the suitability process must have adequate skills, knowledge, and expertise.

- **Periodic Statement Format:**

  - Periodic statements must be provided in a durable medium (e.g., electronic format).

  - If an online system is used, specific requirements apply regarding access, notification, and availability of information.

  - The periodic statement must include specific content, such as portfolio valuation, performance, fees, comparison with benchmarks, transaction details, and an explanation of how activities meet client preferences.

**What it means for firms:**

- **Enhanced Client Onboarding and Suitability Assessment:**

  - Firms must review and improve their client onboarding procedures to collect the necessary information for suitability assessments in compliance with the guidelines.

  - This may involve redesigning questionnaires, implementing systems to verify client information, and providing staff training.

- **Product Governance and Due Diligence:**

  - Firms must establish robust product governance frameworks to understand and classify crypto-assets, assess their risks and complexity, and ensure they suit clients.

  - This will require ongoing monitoring of crypto-asset markets.

- **Portfolio Management Practices:**

  - Firms providing portfolio management services must implement procedures for assessing the suitability of investment decisions, considering the costs and benefits of switching, and ensuring appropriate portfolio diversification.

- **Technology and Systems:**

  - Firms may need to invest in technology to support compliance with the guidelines, such as systems for client data management, suitability assessment tools, and periodic statement generation.

- **Staff Training and Competence:**

  - Firms must ensure that staff involved in the suitability process have the necessary skills, knowledge, and expertise, which will require ongoing training.

- **Documentation and Record Keeping:**

  - Firms must maintain adequate documentation and records to comply with the guidelines, including suitability assessments, investment decisions, and client communications.

- **Ongoing Compliance and Monitoring:**

  - Firms must establish procedures for monitoring ongoing compliance and reviewing client information regularly to ensure that it remains up-to-date.

- **Increased Transparency and Reporting:**

  - Firms will need to provide clients with clear and comprehensive information about the suitability assessment process and the performance of their portfolios.

- **Cost Implications:**

  - Compliance with the guidelines may involve significant costs for firms, including investments in technology, staff training, and enhanced compliance procedures.

In summary, these ESMA guidelines under MiCA will require crypto-asset service providers offering advice or portfolio management to change their operations substantially. The focus is on robust client protection, and firms must prioritise compliance to avoid regulatory scrutiny and penalties.

### MiCA: Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/421 regarding RTS specifying the data necessary for classifying crypto asset white papers and the practical arrangements to ensure such data is machine-readable. The Delegated Regulation reflects a mandate in Article 109(8) of MiCA.

This Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/421 supplements the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA) (EU) 2023/1114, specifying the data necessary for the classification of crypto-asset white papers and the practical arrangements to ensure that such data is machine-readable. Here's a summary and the firm implications:

**Summary:**

- **Purpose:**

  - To standardise the data required for classifying crypto-asset white papers within the register established under MiCA.

  - To ensure that this data is machine-readable for efficient processing and accessibility.

- **Data Requirements:**

  - Specifies the data fields for classifying white papers, including issuer information, crypto-asset identifiers, and publication details.

  - Mandates the use of the ISO 20022 standard for data formatting.

  - Allows for using the same machine-readable format as the white paper itself if the data is included.

  - Mandates the usage of legal entity identifiers(LEI).

  - It mandates the usage of ISO 24165 Digital Token Identifiers (DTI) and, where not available, digital token identifiers approved by ESMA.

- **Machine-Readability:**

  - Aims to facilitate automated processing and analysis of white paper data.

  - Aligns with the data requirements for the European Single Access Point (ESAP).

- **Crypto-Asset Identification:**

  - Uses the ISO 24165 Digital Token Identifiers (DTI) to identify crypto-assets uniquely.

  - Where ISO 24165 DTI is unavailable, the regulation mandates using an ESMA-approved digital token identifier.

- **Implementation:**

  - Applies from 23 December 2025.

  - Aligns its application date with Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2024/2984.

**What it means for firms:**

- **Data Standardization:**

  - Issuers must ensure their white papers include the specified data fields in the required format (ISO 20022).

  - This standardisation will increase the consistency and comparability of crypto-asset information.

- **Machine-Readable Formats:**

  - Issuers must adapt their data preparation processes to ensure machine-readability, potentially requiring investment in data management tools.

  - This will facilitate automated reporting and compliance.

- **Legal Entity Identifiers (LEIs):**

  - Issuers must obtain and maintain valid LEIs, adding to their administrative overhead.

  - This reinforces the identification and accountability of legal entities in the crypto-asset space.

- **Digital Token Identifiers (DTIs):**

  - Issuers must incorporate DTIs into their white papers, requiring technical expertise and coordination with relevant standards bodies.

  - This will improve the tracking and classification of crypto-assets.

- **Increased Transparency:**

  - The regulation enhances the transparency of crypto-asset information, potentially boosting investor confidence.

  - This may lead to greater scrutiny of issuer disclosures.

- **Compliance Costs:**

  - Firms will face costs associated with data standardisation, machine readability, and the implementation of identifiers.

  - These costs should be factored into operational budgets.

- **Technical adaptation:**

  - Firms must adapt their technical infrastructure to comply with the machine-readable data formats.

- **Preparation time:**

  - Firms will have to prepare for the application date of 23 December 2025.

This regulation mandates a significant shift towards standardised, machine-readable data for crypto-asset white papers to improve transparency and regulatory oversight.

### MiCA: Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/419 regarding RTS specifying the procedure and timeframe for an issuer of ARTs or e-money tokens to adjust the amount of its funds. The Delegated Regulation reflects a mandate in Article 45(7) of MiCA

This Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/419 supplements the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA) (EU) 2023/1114, specifying the procedure and timeframe for issuers of asset-referenced tokens (ARTs) or e-money tokens (EMTs) to adjust their funds. Here's a summary and the firm implications:

**Summary:**

- **Scope:**

  - Applies to issuers of significant ARTs and EMTs.

  - It also applies to issuers of non-significant ARTs and EMTs when required by competent authorities.

- **Adjustment of Own Funds:**

  - Issuers must develop and implement a plan to adjust their funds to the required level within a specified timeframe.

  - The competent authority of the home Member State sets the timeframe following a dialogue with the issuer.

  - The maximum timeframe for adjustment is 6 months from the notification.

  - Issuers must submit a detailed plan outlining time-bound steps and procedures for the adjustment.

  - Competent authorities closely monitor the implementation of the plan.

  - Issuers must promptly notify of any deviations from the plan and provide updates.

  - Issuers must inform the competent authority upon completion of the adjustment.

- **Purpose:**

  - Ensure issuers maintain adequate funds to mitigate risks and protect token holders.

  - To maintain financial stability within the crypto-asset market.

**What it means for firms:**

- **Rapid Adjustment Requirements:**

  - Issuers must be prepared to adjust their funds within a relatively short time (maximum 6 months), requiring efficient capital management.

  - This necessitates proactive planning and access to liquid assets.

- **Detailed Adjustment Plans:**

  - Issuers must develop comprehensive adjustment plans with clear steps and timelines, increasing administrative burden.

  - These plans must demonstrate compliance with regulatory requirements regarding eligible funds instruments.

- **Increased Regulatory Oversight:**

  - Competent authorities will closely monitor the adjustment process, requiring frequent communication and transparency.

  - Firms should be prepared for more intensive regulatory scrutiny.

- **Operational and Financial Flexibility:**

  - Issuers must maintain operational and financial flexibility to respond to adjustment requirements promptly.

  - This may involve diversifying funding sources and maintaining adequate liquidity reserves.

- **Potential Impact on Capital Planning:**

  - Firms must always have very robust capital planning procedures to be prepared to increase their funds.

- **Increased reporting requirements:**

  - Firms will need to increase their reporting to the competent authorities.

- **Risk of penalties:**

  - Failure to comply with the timeframes and procedures could result in penalties.

In summary, this regulation imposes strict timelines and reporting requirements on ART and EMT issuers regarding adjusting their funds, forcing firms to maintain robust capital management and close communication with regulatory authorities.

### MiCA: Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/418 regarding RTS specifying the minimum content of the governance arrangements on the remuneration policy of issuers of significant ARTs or e-money tokens. The Delegated Regulation reflects a mandate in Article 45(7) of MiCA

This Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2025/418 supplements the Markets in Crypto-Assets Regulation (MiCA) (EU) 2023/1114, specifying the minimum content of governance arrangements for remuneration policies of issuers of significant asset-referenced tokens (ARTs) or e-money tokens (EMTs). Here's a summary and the firm implications:

- **Scope:**

  - Applies to issuers of significant ARTs and EMTs.

  - It also applies to issuers of non-significant ARTs and EMTs when required by competent authorities.

- **Remuneration Policy Governance:**

  - Requires issuers to establish and maintain sound remuneration policies.

  - Management bodies are responsible for approving and overseeing these policies.

  - Policies must align with sound risk management, the issuer's long-term interests, and the token holders' protection.

  - Policies are required to contain ESG risk factors.

- **Remuneration Principles:**

  - Policies must be consistent with the rights and interests of token holders, gender-neutral, and aligned with the issuer's risk appetite.

  - Control function staff must be remunerated based on control objectives, independent of business unit performance.

  - Variable remuneration must be linked to sustainable and risk-adjusted performance.

  - Limits on the ratio of variable to fixed remuneration are mandated.

  - Portions of variable remuneration must be deferred and paid in instruments (shares, tokens, etc.).

  - Malus and clawback clauses are required.

- **Identification of Staff:**

  - Defines "identified staff" as those with a material impact on the issuer's risk profile or the risk profile of the tokens.

  - Sets criteria for identifying such staff, including management, control functions, and key risk management roles.

- **ESG Integration:**

  - Explicitly requires remuneration policies to consider environmental, social, and governance (ESG) risk factors, particularly climate-related risks from IT infrastructure and consensus mechanisms.

**What it means for firms:**

- **Significant Policy Overhaul:**

  - Issuers must develop or revise their remuneration policies to comply with detailed requirements, requiring substantial effort and resources.

  - This includes implementing robust governance frameworks and ensuring policies are well-documented and transparent.

- **Increased Regulatory Scrutiny:**

  - Competent authorities will closely monitor remuneration practices, increasing the compliance burden.

  - Firms must be prepared for detailed audits and demonstrate adherence to the new standards.

- **Risk Management Alignment:**

  - Remuneration policies must be tightly aligned with risk management objectives, requiring a fundamental shift in evaluating performance.

  - This necessitates the development of sophisticated risk assessment methodologies and performance metrics.

- **Variable Remuneration Restrictions:**

  - Limits on variable remuneration and deferral requirements will impact compensation structures, potentially affecting talent attraction and retention.

  - The requirement to pay a significant portion of variable remuneration in instruments adds complexity to compensation planning.

- **Enhanced Control Function Independence:**

  - Stricter rules on control function remuneration reinforce their independence and importance, requiring clear separation from business units.

- **ESG Integration Costs:**

  - Integrating ESG factors into remuneration policies will increase compliance costs and require expertise in ESG risk assessment.

  - Firms will need to develop systems to track and measure ESG performance.

- **Operational changes:**

  - Firms must change operational procedures to document and comply with the new rules.

### MiCA: Official translations of ESMA guidelines on conditions for qualification of crypto assets as financial instruments under MiCA

- **Purpose:**

  - These guidelines clarify the distinction between crypto-assets regulated under MiCA and those under existing financial instrument regulations (MiFID II).

- **Scope:**

  - Applies to national competent authorities (NCAs) and all financial market participants involved with crypto-assets.

  - Focuses on Article 2(5) of MiCA.

- **Key Principles:**

  - **Technology Neutrality:** The technological format of a crypto-asset (e.g., tokenisation) does not determine its classification.

  - **Substance over Form:** The underlying economic reality of a crypto-asset determines its classification, not just its label.

  - **Case-by-Case Analysis:** Each crypto-asset must be assessed individually.

- **Classification Criteria:**

  - **Transferable Securities:** Crypto-assets are classified as transferable securities if they confer equivalent rights to shares, bonds, or other MiFID II transferable securities. Key criteria include:

    - Exclusion of instruments of payment.

    - Being "classes of securities" (interchangeable, same issuer).

    - Being negotiable on the capital market.

  - **Other Financial Instruments:** Guidelines also address classification as money-market instruments, units in collective investment undertakings, derivative contracts, and emission allowances.

  - **NFTs:** Unique and non-fungible crypto-assets (NFTs) are generally outside MiCA's scope but can be classified as financial instruments if they meet relevant criteria. Fractionalised NFTs require special attention.

  - **Hybrid Tokens:** Hybrid tokens, possessing characteristics of financial instruments and other crypto-assets, must be primarily assessed for financial instrument classification.

- **Compliance and Reporting:**

  - NCAs must notify ESMA of their compliance status by May 18, 2025.

  - Financial market participants are not required to report compliance.

  - The guidelines will apply from May 18, 2025.

**What it means for firms:**

- **Increased Regulatory Clarity:** The guidelines provide much-needed clarity for crypto-asset issuers, service providers, and investors, reducing regulatory uncertainty.

- **Potential for Increased MiFID II Scrutiny:** Crypto assets with characteristics similar to traditional financial instruments will likely face increased scrutiny and regulation under MiFID II.

- **Impact on the NFT Market:** The guidelines focus on the "interdependent value test" and fractionalised NFTs, which will significantly impact the NFT market and potentially lead to more regulatory oversight.

- **Compliance Burden:** Financial market participants must conduct thorough assessments of their crypto assets to ensure compliance with the guidelines, potentially increasing their compliance burden.

- **Need for Legal Expertise:** Firms will need legal expertise to navigate the complex classification criteria and ensure compliance.

- **Market Adjustments:** The guidelines may lead to market adjustments as some crypto-assets are reclassified and subject to stricter regulations.

- **National Competent Authority Action:** NCAs must update their supervisory frameworks, changing how crypto businesses operate within each member state.

- **Blurring lines:** The guidelines highlight that the lines between crypto assets and traditional financial instruments are blurring and that firms must be aware that simply calling something a crypto asset does not exclude it from traditional financial regulations.

### CBDC: ECB - Working Paper Series Consumer attitudes towards a central bank digital currency

- **Addressing Adoption Barriers:** Policymakers must address the significant role of habit persistence in payment preferences. Many consumers are reluctant to switch from existing payment methods. Communication strategies should acknowledge and attempt to overcome this inertia.

- **Limited Impact on Wealth Allocation:** The introduction of a digital euro may have a limited impact on how consumers allocate wealth across different financial assets. Consumers will likely allocate only a tiny portion of new funds to it.

- **Holding Limits and Financial Stability:** Holding limits within a reasonable range may not significantly affect the composition of consumer liquid asset portfolios, suggesting that concerns about large-scale disintermediation of banks due to varying holding limits might be less severe than feared, at least within the ranges tested.

- **Offline Functionality:** Highlighting features similar to cash, such as offline functionality, can encourage adoption.

- **Limitations:**

- **Darknet Market and Fraud Shop Decline:** Darknet markets received \$2 billion (down from \$2.3 billion in 2023), and fraud shop volume declined by slightly more than half to \$220.1 million, partly due to law enforcement takedowns.

### IMF-Privacy Technologies & The Digital Economy: A Primer for Supervisors

This IMF Working Paper provides a primer on privacy technologies for financial supervisors, focusing on their role in balancing the benefits and risks of the digital economy. Here's a summary and the firm implications:

- **The Digital Economy and Privacy:**

  - The digital economy relies on big data, creating opportunities and significant privacy risks.

  - Privacy concerns can deter users from participating in the digital economy, hindering innovation and growth.

  - Privacy technologies, alongside robust regulation, can build trust and enable secure data sharing.

- **Privacy Technologies:**

  - The paper categorises privacy technologies into input (reducing data access) and output (reducing inference from shared data).

  - **Input technologies:**

    - Homomorphic encryption: Allows computations on encrypted data.

    - Secure multiparty computation (SMPC): Enables joint computations without revealing individual data.

    - Federated learning: Trains machine learning models on decentralised data.

  - **Output technologies:**

    - Zero-knowledge proofs (ZKPs): Verify information without revealing the information itself.

    - Data masking: Obfuscates or replaces sensitive data.

    - Differential privacy: Adds noise to datasets to protect individual privacy.

    - Synthetic data: Generates artificial data that mimics accurate data.

- **Considerations for Supervisors:**

  - **Understanding trade-offs:** Supervisors must understand the strengths and weaknesses of privacy technologies and their implications.

  - **Coordination and cooperation:** Domestic and international collaboration is crucial for knowledge sharing and regulatory clarity.

  - **Cybersecurity implications:** Supervisors must understand the cybersecurity risks and trade-offs associated with privacy technologies.

**What it means for firms:**

- **Regulatory Awareness and Adaptation:**

  - Financial supervisors must proactively engage with emerging privacy technologies to understand their impact on financial markets.

  - Existing regulations may need to be adapted, or new rules may need to be developed to address the unique challenges posed by these technologies.<sup>1</sup>

  - Supervisors should promote the responsible use of these technologies, balancing innovation with consumer protection.

- **Enhanced Supervision and Risk Management:**

  - Supervisors should incorporate the assessment of privacy technologies into their risk management frameworks.

  - They must know the potential for a "false sense of security" and ensure robust cybersecurity measures are in place.

  - Supervisors need to be able to assess the implications of using synthetic data and the risks involved with its use.

- **International Cooperation and Knowledge Sharing:**

  - Financial authorities should actively participate in international forums to share best practices and develop harmonised regulatory approaches.

  - Cooperation agreements and information-sharing mechanisms are essential for addressing cross-border risks.

- **Promoting Trust and Data Protection:**

  - Supervisors play a key role in fostering trust in the digital economy by ensuring adequate data protection and privacy.

  - They should encourage the adoption of privacy-enhancing technologies that empower users and protect their data.

- **Technology Neutrality vs. Targeted Approaches:**

  - While technology neutrality is often cited, supervisors may need to adopt targeted approaches for certain privacy technologies that pose unique risks or require specific regulatory responses.

  - This is especially true regarding technologies that heavily impact financial integrity and that impact AML/CFT.

- **Resource and Expertise Development:**

  - Financial authorities must invest in developing the necessary expertise and resources to understand and supervise privacy technologies.

  - This may involve training staff, hiring specialists, and collaborating with academic and industry experts.

- **Policy Implications:**

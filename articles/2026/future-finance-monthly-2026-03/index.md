## Executive Summary

**Macro Assessment:**

The global regulatory environment has definitively shifted from policy formulation to aggressive jurisdictional enforcement. For global Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASPs), the era of borderless regulatory arbitrage and vertically integrated "super-app" models is over. Survival and profitability in 2026 and beyond require a structural pivot toward institutional-grade operational resilience, localised licensing, and strict functional segregation.

Below are the most material impacts on CASP business models and revenue generation.

#### 1. Structural Business Model Shifts

- **The Forced Unbundling of the "Super-App" (Global/FSB):** The most existential threat to the current CASP business model is the global enforcement of the FSB’s rules on Multi-Function Crypto-Asset Intermediaries (MCIs). Jurisdictions are now heavily penalising the commingling of proprietary trading, retail exchanges, and custody.

  - *Impact:* We must fundamentally restructure operations. Vertically integrated models must be spun out into distinct, bankruptcy-remote corporate entities with strict informational firewalls.

- **The Death of Offshore Arbitrage (FATF/UK):** Global standard-setters and national FIUs are aggressively targeting offshore Virtual Asset Service Providers (oVASPs). Initiatives such as the UK's FSMA authorisation gateway and the FATF's mandate against "reverse solicitation" mean that serving tier-1 markets without local, full-scope financial services licenses is no longer viable.

  - *Impact:* Customer acquisition models relying on third-party marketing approvers or offshore liquidity hubs will be systematically choked off by traditional banking partners and IP-blocking mandates.

- **The U.S. Market Legitimisation (SEC/CFTC/Congress):** The U.S. is rapidly transitioning from "regulation by enforcement" to statutory clarity. The SEC/CFTC joint token taxonomy and the advancement of FIT21 provide a legally durable framework for digital commodities.

  - *Impact:* This allows us to transition from ad-hoc legal defence strategies to standardised, controls-based operations in the U.S., paving the way for dual SEC/CFTC registration and the safe relisting of high-volume digital assets.

#### 2. Material Impacts on Revenue and Capital

- **Explosion in Compliance OPEX and Tech Debt:** Across the UK, Singapore, Thailand, and the US, regulators are mandating real-time, API-driven compliance. The FATF’s zero-tolerance stance on the Travel Rule, combined with mandates for "rapid transaction freezing" and AI-driven vendor risk management (DORA/MAS TPRM), requires massive technology upgrades.

  - *Impact:* Sustained, high-level capital expenditure is required for enterprise-grade blockchain analytics, real-time FIU intelligence sharing, and the complete overhaul of third-party vendor SLAs.

- **Margin Compression on Stablecoins and Retail Trading:** The IMF’s empirical validation of stablecoin macroeconomic risks guarantees tighter prudential constraints. Concurrently, strict UI/UX consumer-duty rules (e.g., the UK FCA) will ban gamified trading.

  - *Impact:* Yield generated from stablecoin backing assets will compress as regulators force issuers into unremunerated central bank deposits or ultra-short-term sovereign debt. Furthermore, introducing friction in retail onboarding will reduce impulse-driven transaction velocity and associated fee revenue.

- **Unlocking Institutional "Tokenisation-as-a-Service" (TaaS) Revenue:** The Bank of Canada’s successful W-CAD (wholesale CBDC) bond settlement and the U.S. SEC’s incoming "innovation exemption" for atomic settlement validate the institutional use of public ledgers.

  - *Impact:* Highly positive. This opens a massive, net-new B2B revenue stream. Compliant CASPs can now safely commercialise their proprietary custody and smart-contract settlement infrastructure to legacy financial institutions (e.g., tier-1 banks, asset managers) for Real-World Asset (RWA) tokenisation.

#### Strategic Imperatives for the Board

1. **Fund the Corporate Restructure:** Immediately allocate capital to model the financial impact of spinning out custody and proprietary trading into standalone licensed entities to preempt the global MCI crackdown.

2. **Pivot to Institutional B2B Sales:** Offset retail margin compression by aggressively marketing our Tokenisation-as-a-Service (TaaS) and smart-contract infrastructure to traditional financial institutions, leveraging the new sovereign and SEC validations.

3. **Approve the U.S. Expansion Budget:** Capitalise on the U.S. deregulatory window. Fund the immediate cross-referencing of our token inventory against the new SEC/CFTC taxonomy to safely aggressively expand our U.S. spot listings and capture institutional market share.

### MiCAR: Draft RTS on complaints handling under MiCAR - Final report

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** EU / European Banking Authority (EBA)

**Source:** [eba.europa.eu](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2024-03/22e7f584-a1d9-44eb-8b07-ab0bdbb3b4d2/Draft%20RTS%20on%20complaints%20handling%20under%20MiCAR.pdf)

**Summary:** The EBA published its final Draft Regulatory Technical Standards (RTS) outlining the requirements, templates, and procedures for complaints handling by Issuers of Asset-Referenced Tokens (IARTs) and third-party distributing entities under MiCA. **What it means for firms:** CASPs and stablecoin issuers distributing ARTs in the EU must immediately overhaul their customer support and dispute resolution frameworks to meet these specific RTS requirements. **Next Steps:** Compliance and operations teams must review the finalised RTS and integrate the mandatory complaint-filing templates and response timelines into their customer service workflows. Systems for recording and investigating complaints must be audited to ensure complete alignment with EBA standards before the MiCA application date for ARTs.

**Timeline:** Published 13 March 2024. Requirements apply from 30 June 2024 (when MiCA provisions for ARTs take effect).

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality:** Changes required. Firms acting as third-party distributors for ARTs must establish formal procedures to facilitate complaint handling between token holders and issuers and update their Service Level Agreements (SLAs).

### DORA: Compliance table on the Joint Guidelines on costs and losses under DORA

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** EU / ESMA & ESAs

This crystallises the mandatory, standardised methodologies that financial entities, including CASPs, must use to quantify and report financial impacts stemming from cyber and IT incidents.

**What it means for firms:** With DORA now in force, CASPs are legally bound by their respective NCAs to calculate the financial impact of any IT outage or cyberattack using the rigid EU formulas. **Timeline:** Published 13 March 2026; active enforcement underway.

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality:** Changes required. Cyber incident response can no longer be handled solely by the IT department; the finance department must be structurally integrated into the triage process to calculate regulatory metrics for costs and losses accurately.

### Payments/ MiCA: Opinion of the European Banking Authority on the supervisory priorities at the end of the transition period under the EBA’s No Action letter on the interplay between PSD2 and MiCA

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** EU / EBA

**Source:** [eba.europa.eu](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-02/3b8b6f18-ca26-4ce1-83eb-d060276f3301/Opinion%20on%20the%20end%20of%20the%20NAL%20transition%20period.pdf)

**Summary:** As of 2 March 2026, the transition period established by the EBA’s No-Action Letter regarding the overlap of PSD2 and MiCA officially ended. National Competent Authorities are now instructed to aggressively enforce PSD2 authorisation requirements for CASPs that execute Electronic Money Token (EMT) transfers, as these constitute regulated payment services. However, the EBA’s February 2026 Opinion provides specific relief under “Scenario 2,” advising NCAs to allow CASPs with pending PSD2 applications to continue carrying out EMT transactions, provided certain supervisory conditions are met.

**What it means for firms:** Any CASP operating in the EU that facilitates stablecoin payouts, transfers, or merchant settlements without a parallel PSD2 Payment Service Provider (PSP) license—or without acting as a registered agent of one—is now operating non-compliantly. The regulatory tolerance has evaporated, exposing unauthorised firms to immediate orders to cease business and severe penalties.

**Next Steps:** IRMs lacking proper authorisation must immediately freeze any non-compliant EMT transfer services to avoid enforcement action. Entities with pending PSD2 applications may continue temporarily, contrary to prior assumptions, and are not subject to a blanket mandate requiring them to completely freeze marketing or client onboarding for these functionalities, provided they meet the EBA’s ongoing supervisory conditions.

**Timeline:** The transition period formally expired on 2 March 2026, triggering immediate enforcement mandates across the EU.

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality:** Changes required. Firms must definitively ring-fence their stablecoin transfer and custodial payout architectures to ensure they do not illegally trigger PSD2 payment execution rules without the correct licensing. Processing withdrawals or facilitating first-party transfers between wallets using EMTs will now trigger unauthorised payment service violations.

- **Materiality:** Changes required. Enterprise-wide AML risk assessments, customer onboarding flows, and automated transaction monitoring algorithms must be fundamentally updated to prevent compliance breaches under the heightened requirements.

**Source:** [sec.gov](http://sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-26-sec-cftc-announce-historic-memorandum-understanding-between-agencies)

**Summary:** On 11 March 2026, the SEC and CFTC announced a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) establishing a Joint Harmonisation Initiative. The agreement aims to clarify definitions of digital asset products, modernise clearing frameworks, and provide a unified regulatory structure for dually registered platforms.

**What it means for firms:** This signals a structural shift towards unified federal oversight, mitigating conflicting compliance burdens for platforms offering both digital commodities and tokenised securities. The initiative paves the way for a “super-app” model in the US, allowing firms to consolidate trading operations and leverage capital-efficient cross-margining.

**Next Steps:** Legal and Regulatory Affairs teams must actively monitor the outputs of the Joint Harmonisation Initiative. The General Counsel should prepare a feasibility study on dual SEC/CFTC registration to capitalise on future substituted compliance frameworks.

**Timeline: 11 March 2026:** MOU signed, and Joint Harmonisation Initiative launched.

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality:** Changes required. Dual-registered entities will need to consolidate their compliance architecture to align with the new unified federal standards. This presents a major structural opportunity to unify security and commodity token trading.

### Digital Assets: SEC Formally Withdraws Proposed Amendments to “Custody Rule” for Digital Assets

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** USA / Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)

**Source:** [sec.gov](http://sec.gov/rules-regulations/2025/06/safeguarding-advisory-client-assets)

**Summary:** The SEC’s “Safeguarding Advisory Client Assets” rule (originally proposed in February 2023) was formally withdrawn in June 2025 following a change in the SEC’s administration and leadership. The proposal, which would have expanded the definition of client assets to include “all crypto assets” and imposed draconian “qualified custodian” requirements, is no longer active as a rulemaking effort.

**What it means for firms:** The existential barrier for non-bank CASPs created by the proposed rule has been completely removed. Institutional crypto-custodians no longer face the imminent threat of needing to hold a state or federal bank charter to serve institutional RIAs. However, RIAs must still comply with the existing 1940 Act custody rule (Rule 206(4)-2).

**Next Steps:** Firms can stand down from emergency contingency planning related to the “Safeguarding” proposal. Compliance and legal teams should pivot to ensuring strict adherence to existing custody guidelines while monitoring for any new, industry-friendly regulatory frameworks emerging from the current SEC. Furthermore, legal teams must actively leverage the SEC Division of Investment Management’s late-2025 No-Action Letter, which explicitly clarified that federally registered investment advisers can utilise state-chartered trust companies to hold clients’ crypto assets, establishing a major new compliance pathway.

**Timeline:February 2023:** Rule Proposed.

**June 2025:** Rule Formally Withdrawn.

**Current Status:** Dead; existing Rule 206(4)-2 remains in effect.

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality:** Reduced. Pure-play software custody providers are no longer at imminent risk of losing RIA clients due to a lack of bank charter status.

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** Global Standards / IMF

**Source:** [elibrary.imf.org](http://elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2026/044/article-A000-en.pdf)

**Summary:** In March 2026, the IMF published a working paper evaluating the causal effects of stablecoin adoption on U.S. financial markets. The research provides empirical evidence that stablecoin demand acts as a novel channel for asset-market transmission, persistently lowering short-term Treasury yields, depreciating the U.S. dollar, and creating gradual spillovers into broader crypto and equity markets.

**What it means for firms:** This report firmly establishes stablecoins as systemic macroeconomic instruments with measurable impacts on sovereign debt markets, rather than niche crypto assets. For CASPs, this empirical validation by the IMF will inevitably accelerate global regulatory efforts by the FSB and BCBS to impose strict, bank-like reserve and prudential requirements on stablecoin issuers.

**Next Steps:** Policy and strategy teams should review the IMF’s empirical findings to anticipate upcoming regulatory pressure on stablecoin reserves and prepare advocacy strategies accordingly.

**Timeline:**

- **March 2026:** Working paper published, informing upcoming global standard-setting agendas.

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality:** Medium. Forecasts tighter prudential constraints and reserve requirements for stablecoin issuers globally.

### FinCrime: New FATF report highlights illicit finance risks linked to Offshore VASPs

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** Global Standards / FATF

**Source:** [fatf-gafi.org](http://fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Virtualassets/Understanding-Mitigating-Risks-Offshore-VASPs.html)

**Summary:** On 11 March 2026, the FATF published a targeted report outlining how gaps in the regulation of offshore Virtual Asset Service Providers (oVASPs) are being exploited for large-scale illicit finance. The report urges member jurisdictions to adopt an “activity-based” approach to regulation—capturing services provided into their markets by offshore entities regardless of physical presence—and explicitly recommends that financial institutions refrain from dealing with unregistered oVASPs.

**What it means for firms:** This establishes a coordinated global mandate against “reverse solicitation” and offshore-only operational models. Global CASPs operating without local licenses or utilising nested corporate structures will face immediate disruptions as traditional banking partners sever correspondent relationships to avoid sanctions risks.

**Next Steps:** Legal and Compliance teams must urgently audit the firm’s global operational footprint and map the regulatory status of all offshore entities. Furthermore, they must review B2B client relationships to identify and remediate any nested oVASP exposure.

**Timeline:**

- **11 March 2026:** Report and supervisory guidance published.

- **Immediate:** Shift in national enforcement priorities and FIU-to-FIU cooperation globally.

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality:** The global shift toward activity-based regulation means offshore-only liquidity hubs and cross-border regulatory arbitrage strategies are increasingly non-compliant. Firms must transition to localised licensing structures to maintain uninterrupted market access.

### FinCrime: FATF targeted report on stablecoins and unhosted wallets

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** International / FATF (Directly informs UK/EU AML/CFT rules)

**Source:** [fatf-gafi.org](https://www.fatf-gafi.org/en/publications/Virtualassets/targeted-report-stablecoins-unhosted-wallets.html?utm_)

**Summary:** On 3 March 2026, the FATF published a targeted report on mitigating money-laundering, terrorist-financing, and proliferation-financing risks associated with stablecoins and unhosted wallets. Driven by data showing that stablecoins accounted for 84% of illicit virtual asset volume in 2025 and are heavily utilised by state-linked cybercriminal groups, the FATF urges jurisdictions to implement Recommendation 15 fully. The report explicitly encourages jurisdictions to require stablecoin issuers to adopt risk-based technical controls—such as the ability to freeze, burn, or withdraw stablecoins in the secondary market, implement smart contract controls like allow-listing (restricting transactions to pre-approved addresses), and conduct strict customer due diligence (CDD) at redemption.

**What it means for firms:** This signals an impending tightening of global regulations on permissionless stablecoins and peer-to-peer (P2P) transfers. CASPs and stablecoin issuers will face heightened, coordinated supervisory expectations from EU and UK regulators to implement smart contract-level central controls (including allow-listing) and apply strict CDD for unhosted wallet interactions.

**Next Steps:** Engineering and AML teams must review current stablecoin architectures to ensure smart contracts technically permit asset freezing, burning, and allow-listing where legally mandated. Firms must also assess their blockchain analytics tooling to ensure it is sophisticated enough to monitor and flag illicit P2P stablecoin flows to and from unhosted wallets.

**Timeline:**

- Published 3 March 2026. Sets the immediate best-practice standard for upcoming national AML rulemakings and supervisory audits.

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality:** Changes required. Issuers of algorithmic or fully decentralised stablecoins lacking protocol-level “freeze” or “allow-listing” functions may find themselves locked out of FATF-compliant jurisdictions. Exchanges will need to upgrade their on-chain analysis tools to intercept unhosted wallet flows.

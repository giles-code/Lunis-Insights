## Executive Summary

**The month in one line.** The UK finished its guidance but not its carve-outs, the US lost its statute and turned to agencies and courts, US states widened their attack on prediction markets, and the EU’s prudential bodies set out to bring crypto lending and staking inside MiCA.

- **\[High\] European Union — MiCA review.** Before the consultation closes on **30 September**, the EBA answered “Yes” to regulating crypto lending, and the ESCB said staking, lending and borrowing should be regulated at Union level. The ESCB also backs moving all CASP supervision to ESMA and wants significant CASPs to hold an EU intermediate parent.

- **\[High\] United Kingdom.** The FCA published PS26/18, its final perimeter guidance, on 16 September. The perimeter itself is already law in S.I. 2026/102. The Miscellaneous Amendments Regulations, laid on 15 September, add three carve-outs — technical services, UK qualifying stablecoin dealing and payment float — and neither House has approved them. The gateway opens on **30 September**.

- **\[High\] Asia-Pacific — Thailand.** The SEC issued a binding Travel Rule with self-hosted wallet verification, effective 27 February 2027. Its Board approved principles restricting stablecoin transfers to customers’ own wallets; it filed a criminal complaint against the operators of two Web3 apps and an alleged promoter (access blocked from 1 October); and it consulted on net capital. On the opportunity side, it proposed opening overseas crypto derivatives to Thai retail investors.

- **\[Nil\] Middle East and North Africa.** No **High** or **Medium** developments this month. FinCEN’s section 311 proposal on Banque Misr UAE closes for comment on 1 October.

- **\[High\] United States and UK — sanctions and financial crime.** OFAC designated the Xinbi Guarantee marketplace with 52 Tron addresses (9 September) and the Iranian exchange BitBank with **no** addresses (17 September). FinCEN’s scam-centre alert (3 September) named the guarantee-marketplace typology six days before the Xinbi designation. BitBank is an Iran sanctions action. The OFAC annual blocked-property report is due on **30 September**.

- **\[High\] United States — prediction markets.** Connecticut ordered nine platforms, including Coinbase, Crypto.com, Gemini, Robinhood and Webull, to stop offering sports event contracts (10 September). New York sued Polymarket US (24 September). Kalshi lost in Iowa and the Tenth Circuit (8 September) and in the Sixth Circuit (25 September). The CFTC’s only new prediction-market policy was a staff advisory on “mention” contracts (22 September).

- **\[High\] United States — market structure.** Senate cloture on the CLARITY Act (H.R. 3633) failed 49–50 on 15 September. The CFTC sent a market-structure package to OIRA at pre-rule stage two days later. For now, US market structure will be set by agencies and courts, not Congress.

- **\[High, opportunity\] United States — SEC Innovation Exemption.** The SEC exempted qualifying tokenised-stock venues and their liquidity providers from the “exchange” and “dealer” definitions until 17 September 2031 (17 September).

- **\[Low\] International standard setters.** FATF issued typology reports on professional money laundering and on gaming and gambling (3 and 9 September). The G20 Chair’s Statement (1 September) anticipates the FSB’s findings on cross-border stablecoin arrangements. The FATF Paris Plenary meets on 26–30 October.

**Medium, in brief.** The Commission adopted criteria for host-State AML contact points for CASPs (8 September). The ESAs and the EBA tightened DORA incident reporting and third-party risk rules. The Eurosystem launched Pontes. MAS proposed banning issuers from paying interest on MAS-regulated stablecoins. Hong Kong committed to a virtual-asset licensing bill within 2026. Treasury’s GENIUS s.3 proposal, open to 19 October, would make it unlawful from 18 July 2028 for a CASP to offer non-permitted stablecoins to US persons; the Fed became the fourth federal prudential regulator to propose issuer rules (24 September). OSFI finalised its crypto-asset capital guideline with targeted relief.

## European Union

### 1. EU — MiCA review: lending, staking and who supervises CASPs

> **MiCA:** The EBA identifies priorities for the review of MiCA (ESCB response cross-referenced)

- **Jurisdiction/Regulator:** EU — European Banking Authority; European System of Central Banks; European Commission

- **Publication Date:** 24 September 2026 (EBA); ESCB response dated September 2026, reported from 22 September (weekly edition of 26 September)

- **Source Link:** [EBA press release](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-identifies-priorities-review-mica) · [ESCB response](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/consultationresponse/pdf/ecb.conresp202609_micarreview.en.pdf)

**Summary:** The EBA finds the ART and EMT rules “broadly appropriate”. It answers “Yes” (Q67) to regulating crypto lending and borrowing, including where CASPs facilitate access to DeFi lending protocols, subject to cost-benefit analysis. It would strengthen the regime for third-country multi-issuer schemes and review the minimum share of reserves held as deposits. It raises possible powers to require EU intermediate parent undertakings for non-EU groups as one option. The ESCB goes further:

- “Staking, lending and borrowing of crypto-assets should be regulated at Union level” (as quoted in press coverage of 22 September);

- keep the stablecoin remuneration ban and extend it to “both direct and indirect forms of remuneration”, including through CASPs’ lending and staking;

- require significant CASPs to establish an EU intermediate parent undertaking;

- support the Commission’s proposal to move all CASP supervision to ESMA.

As at 1 September 2026, 39 EMTs had been issued under MiCA and no ARTs authorised.

**What it means for firms:** Two structural questions now sit with the board, not the compliance function. First, who supervises: the Commission’s proposal to move CASP authorisation and supervision to ESMA has the ESCB’s support. Second, how the EU group is built: the ESCB would require significant CASPs to hold an EU intermediate parent undertaking; the EBA raises such a power only as one option. On products, both prudential bodies say crypto lending belongs inside MiCA; only the ESCB extends that to staking. None of this is law. ESMA separately set digital innovation, including tokenisation, as a supervisory priority from 2027 (23 September).

**Timeline:** Commission targeted and public consultations close on 30 September 2026, 23:59 CEST. The EBA’s consultation on the MiCA fines methodology closes on 28 September. The date of the Commission’s review report.

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality: Medium**-dated but structural. EU yield and loan products outside MiCA may acquire an authorisation requirement; the EU group may need an intermediate parent; the supervisor may change.

### 2. EU — criteria for AML central contact points for CASPs
> **MiCA:** Commission Delegated Regulation amending Delegated Regulation (EU) 2018/1108 as regards central contact points for crypto-asset service providers

- **Jurisdiction/Regulator:** EU — European Commission (DG FISMA), on EBA draft RTS

- **Publication Date:** Adopted 8 September 2026 (C(2026) 6179 final) (weekly edition of 12 September)

- **Source Link:** [Commission Delegated Regulation C(2026) 6179](https://ec.europa.eu/transparency/documents-register/api/files/C(2026)6179_0/090166e5332041cc)

**Summary:** The act sets criteria for host Member States to require a central contact point from CASPs established there other than through a branch. A host State may require one where the cumulative value of services is expected to exceed, or has exceeded, EUR 3 million per financial year. Two further triggers need no threshold: a risk-based requirement by category of CASP, and an exceptional-case power where the host State has reasonable grounds to suspect high ML/TF risk. The contact point has six specified duties.

**What it means for firms:** The power to require CASP contact points has existed since Regulation (EU) 2023/1113 extended the AML Directive to CASPs; this act sets the criteria. It reaches agents, distributors and local service entities, not cross-border passporting with no local presence. The article is permissive (“may require”), so exposure depends on how many Member States use it. Question for the next edition: how long these criteria will last once the new AML Regulation applies from 10 July 2027.

**Timeline:** Adopted 8 September 2026. Not published in the Official Journal as of 27 September. Enters into force on the twentieth day after publication.

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality:** Affects the EU operating model where the firm uses non-branch establishments.

### 3. EU and Luxembourg — operational resilience, third-party risk and CASP reporting
> **DORA:** DORA incident reporting – operational instructions; The EBA publishes its final Guidelines on the management of third-party risk; Evolution of prudential reporting for payment institutions, electronic money institutions and crypto-asset service providers

- **Jurisdiction/Regulator:** EU — Joint Committee of the ESAs; European Banking Authority; Luxembourg — CSSF

- **Publication Date:** 10, 16 and 18 September 2026 (weekly editions of 12 and 19 September)

- **Source Link:** [ESAs DORA reporting instructions](https://www.esma.europa.eu/document/dora-incident-reporting-operational-instructions) · [EBA third-party risk Guidelines](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-publishes-its-final-guidelines-management-third-party-risk-delivering-more-proportionate-and) · [CSSF reporting notice](https://www.cssf.lu/en/2026/09/evolution-of-prudential-reporting-for-payment-institutions-electronic-money-institutions-and-crypto-asset-service-providers/)

**Summary:**

- **DORA instructions (16 September).** A fourteen-topic table correcting recurring errors in major ICT incident reports, including stable incident identifiers and at least one intermediate report per month until the final report. Agreed with competent authorities, though stated not to be a legal interpretation.

- **EBA third-party risk Guidelines (18 September).** Cover non-ICT third-party arrangements and repeal the 2019 outsourcing Guidelines. Addressees include issuers of asset-referenced tokens, but not CASPs as such. Application dates are placeholders until translations are published.

- **CSSF (10 September).** A project to modernise prudential reporting for payment institutions, e-money institutions and Article 63 MiCAR CASPs, moving it to eDesk. Frequencies run from monthly (funds segregation) to annual. A circular is to follow; eDesk becomes mandatory from 1 April 2027.

**What it means for firms:** MiCA CASPs are DORA financial entities, so the incident instructions apply to them now and will be applied in supervision. The EBA Guidelines reach a CASP group through its EMI, payment institution, investment firm or ART-issuer entities, not its trading or custody entities. Luxembourg is a common MiCA authorisation seat, so its reporting design is a practical benchmark; whether monthly segregation reporting applies to CASPs will be settled by the circular.

**Timeline:** DORA instructions in effect on publication. EBA Guidelines: dates to be fixed on translation. CSSF: testing 1 January – 31 March 2027; eDesk sole channel from 1 April 2027.

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality:** Operational. Q1 2027 resource contention with UK authorisation work is the real risk.

### 4. Tokenisation infrastructure — Pontes, UK wholesale, US transfer agents, Korea and Japan
> **Digital Assets:** Eurosystem brings central bank money to tokenised finance; FS26/1: Tokenisation in wholesale markets; Call for Input: Tokenised gold; SEC Proposes to Modernise Rules for Registered Transfer Agents

- **Jurisdiction/Regulator:** EU — ECB / Eurosystem; UK — FCA (with the Bank of England); US — SEC; Korea — FSC; Japan — FSA

- **Publication Date:** 1–21 September 2026 (all four weekly editions)

- **Source Link:** [ECB press release (Pontes)](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.pr260921~e754847a7b.en.html) · [FCA FS26/1](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/feedback-statements/fs26-1-tokenisation-wholesale-markets) · [SEC transfer-agent proposal](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-81-sec-proposes-modernize-rules-registered-transfer-agents)

**Summary:**

- **Pontes (21 September).** Wholesale tokenised-asset transactions can now settle in central bank money by linking DLT platforms to TARGET Services. The ECB will also invest part of its own funds in tokenised securities settled via Pontes—full implementation expected by 2028. Pontes is not the retail digital euro.

- **FCA (14 September).** FS26/1 reports 123 responses and points to post-trade collateral as the main opportunity. It promises a joint FCA–Bank of England tokenisation roadmap later in 2026 and a consultation on safeguarding relevant specified investment cryptoassets in H1 2027. A separate Call for Input on tokenised gold closes on 23 October.

- **SEC (1 September).** A proposed overhaul of transfer-agent rules, adding a Form TA-2 item on tokenised securities and naming wallet allowlisting. Comments close 3 November.

- **Korea (4 September).** A three-phase tokenisation roadmap from February 2027; the FSC says Phases 2 and 3 remain flexible pending stablecoin legislation.

- **Japan (15 September).** An FIEA definitions Ordinance narrowing the registration perimeter for group-company distribution of digital bonds (in force 16 September); a separate reform on depositary-receipt rights takes effect 5 October.

**What it means for firms:** Central-bank-money settlement in the euro area strengthens the case for EU tokenised securities against stablecoin-settled alternatives, though CASPs will not connect directly. The UK and Hong Kong (entry 8) are shaping tokenised gold rules in the same quarter, so one product design can be tested against both. The US transfer-agent proposal is enabling rather than restrictive.

**Timeline:** 23 October — FCA tokenised gold Call for Input closes. 3 November — SEC transfer-agent comments. H1 2027 — FCA safeguarding consultation. February 2027 — Korea Phase 1. 2028 — Pontes full implementation.

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality: Low** in the near term; strategic for any tokenised-securities plan.

## United Kingdom
### 5. UK — final perimeter guidance, the amending Regulations and the gateway
> **Digital Assets:** PS26/18: Cryptoasset perimeter guidance; The Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2026

- **Jurisdiction/Regulator:** United Kingdom — Financial Conduct Authority; HM Treasury; both Houses of Parliament

- **Publication Date:** Regulations laid 15 September 2026; PS26/18 published 16 September 2026 (weekly editions of 12, 19 and 26 September)

- **Source Link:** [FCA PS26/18](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps26-18-cryptoasset-perimeter-guidance) · [Miscellaneous Amendments Regulations](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2026/9780348287233/contents) · [FCA gateway page](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/new-regime-cryptoasset-regulation/how-gateway-will-operate)

**Summary:** The month ran in three steps.

- **Nil return (12 September).** The FCA had promised, in its 30 June press release, a September policy statement on the cryptoasset perimeter. At 12 September, it had not appeared.

- **Laying and publication (15–16 September).** The Government laid the amending Regulations, a draft affirmative instrument amending S.I. 2026/102. The next day, the FCA published PS26/18. It finalises the Perimeter Guidance (Regulated Cryptoasset Activities) Instrument 2026 (FCA 2026/55), made on 11 September. It inserts a new PERG 18 and lists eight regulated cryptoasset activities, from issuing a qualifying stablecoin (article 9M) to arranging qualifying cryptoasset staking (article 9Z6).

- **Handbook Notice 144 (late September).** It records FCA 2026/55 and four further cryptoasset instruments: fees (FCA 2026/58, in force 30 September save Part 2); financial promotion notifications (FCA 2026/60, 1 October); authorised funds holding cryptoasset exchange-traded notes, up to 10% for retail funds (FCA 2026/57, 28 September); and DEPP (FCA 2026/61, Part 1 from 30 September).

PS26/18 contains four findings that matter commercially:

- there is **no Overseas Persons Exclusion** for regulated cryptoasset activities;

- there is **no technology-provider exclusion** from safeguarding, so MPC and key-sharding models turn on the “requisite degree of control” test;

- lending and borrowing are not standalone activities; they fall into dealing, arranging or safeguarding by substance, whatever the product is called;

- arranging staking excludes the provision of information or analytics alone.

The amending Regulations narrow the perimeter in three places, described in the FCA’s response after PS26/18 paragraph 5.9 and in the explanatory memorandum: technical services from arranging deals; certain UK qualifying stablecoin activities from dealing and arranging; and temporarily holding UK qualifying stablecoins for payment transactions from safeguarding.

**What it means for firms:** The absence of an Overseas Persons Exclusion, together with the new section 418 FSMA deeming cases, reaches trading services, fiat and payment services and yield, earn and staking products in a single provision. The perimeter itself is law in S.I. 2026/102; what is pending is the three carve-outs. The payment-float carve-out matters most to payment gateways and merchant settlement. The Commons adjourned on 15 September until 12 October, and no committee has yet reported. So a firm that needs a carve-out must decide whether to scope its application with or without it. The 07:00 gateway opening versus the 09:00 start of the “relevant application period” in the FCA’s direction of 20 February 2026 matters only to a first-hour filing. Still, the savings provision is tied to that period.

**Timeline:**

- 30 September 2026 — gateway opens (07:00 per the gateway page; relevant application period from 09:00 per the FCA direction).

- 12 October 2026 — the Commons returns; approval of the Regulations pending. CP26/32 feedback deadline (cryptoasset chapter on admission deferral and execution venues).

- October 2026 — FCA consultation on PERG amendments reflecting the Regulations (“early Q4 2026” in PS26/18); final guidance early 2027.

- 28 February 2027 — relevant application period ends (23:59).

- 25 October 2027 — regime commences.

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality: High** and immediate. Existing FSMA, MLR, PSR and EMR permissions do not convert (PS26/18 paragraph 1.25). Any entity serving UK individuals from offshore needs a section 418 analysis now. The risk is scoping an application to activities the carve-outs may remove, then varying permissions downwards.

## Asia-Pacific
### 6. Thailand — binding Travel Rule, stablecoin controls and a derivatives opening
> **FinCrime:** SEC issues Travel Rule for Digital Assets; SEC Board approves principles for enhancing the supervision of stablecoin transactions; related enforcement and consultations

- **Jurisdiction/Regulator:** Thailand — Securities and Exchange Commission

- **Publication Date:** 31 August – 24 September 2026 (weekly editions of 5, 12 and 26 September)

- **Source Link:** [SEC Thailand Travel Rule release](https://www.sec.or.th/EN/Pages/News_Detail.aspx?SECID=13277) · [SEC Thailand stablecoin principles](https://www.sec.or.th/EN/Pages/News_Detail.aspx?SECID=13294)

**Summary:**

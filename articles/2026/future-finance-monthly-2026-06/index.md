## Executive Summary

**Material Impacts of June Regulatory Developments on CASP Business Models**

**Overview**

Regulatory developments across major global jurisdictions in June reflect a decisive shift from baseline registration frameworks toward comprehensive, institutional-grade supervision of prudential and market structures. For Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASPs), the era of regulatory arbitrage and borderless, capital-light expansion is ending.

The regulatory actions of June will severely compress retail margins, exponentially increase compliance and technology risk expenditures, and force structural changes to core product offerings. Below is an analysis of the most material impacts on CASP business models and revenue streams, categorised by strategic themes.

**1. Structural Unbundling and Capital Squeezes (APAC focus)**

Regulators are aggressively targeting the vertically integrated models favoured by offshore crypto exchanges, mandating structural segregation and higher capital reserves.

- **Key Catalysts:** Hong Kong’s (HKMA) strict guidance on VA dealing financing, shared order books, and custody; Thailand’s (SECT) revisions to digital asset custody regulations and net capital requirements.

- **Materiality:** The global "shared order book" model is under severe threat. CASPs may be forced into ring-fenced, localised liquidity models, drastically reducing market depth and increasing slippage. Furthermore, higher net capital requirements and mandatory custody segregation will trap liquid assets on balance sheets. This significantly reduces Return on Equity (ROE), eliminates internal yield-generation on user assets, and will force many CASPs to outsource custody to institutional third parties.

**2. The Disruption of Stablecoin Economics**

Stablecoins—the primary quote asset and settlement vehicle for CASP trading—are being subjected to bank-like regulatory perimeters, fundamentally altering their unit economics.

- **Key Catalysts:** The US Proposed Rule on a Permitted Payment Stablecoin Issuer Customer Identification Program (CIP); the UK BoE policy on systemic stablecoins; Hong Kong’s (SFC/HKMA) circular on Relevant Stablecoin services; BIS Working Papers on stablecoin anatomy.

- **Materiality:** The US CIP proposal threatens to remove the frictionless, pseudonymous nature of stablecoin issuance. If issuers are forced to implement strict AML/KYC at the token level, CASPs will face delayed settlements, massive spikes in customer data management costs, and increased friction in fiat on- and off-ramps. CASPs will also face heightened liability for auditing their treasury reserves to ensure they interact only with compliant stablecoins.

**3. Existential Threats to Offshore Derivatives vs TradFi Integration**

Regulators are moving to capture the most lucrative revenue streams for global exchanges—perpetual futures and DeFi integrations—under traditional financial (TradFi) rules, while also opening the door to wholesale institutional products.

- **Key Catalysts:** US SEC/CFTC joint push to harmonise Dodd-Frank swap data reporting and derivative product definitions to accommodate Crypto/DeFi; US CFTC review of 24/7 trading and perpetual contracts; Hong Kong HKEX/HKMA pilot using e-HKD for after-hours margin payments for derivatives.

- **Materiality:** Firms offering perpetual futures and unbacked leverage in the US or overlapping jurisdictions face significant regulatory risk unless they build prohibitively expensive clearing infrastructure. However, institutional CASPs stand to gain: Hong Kong’s e-HKD pilot demonstrates a lucrative path forward for B2B firms capable of bridging TradFi settlement (CBDCs) with digital asset market margin requirements.

**4. Transition Cliffs and Quantifiable Enforcement Risks**

Regulators are eliminating grey areas, forcing CASPs into a binary choice: either heavily capitalise to meet comprehensive standards or initiate formal market exits.

- **Key Catalysts:** Europe’s ESMA statement on MiCA transitional periods and wind-down activities; Europe’s EBA consultation on methodologies for setting MiCA fines; Australia’s ASIC extending its no-action position for digital assets to September 2026.

- **Materiality:** The era of operating under transitional ambiguity in the EU is over. ESMA’s explicit guidance requires underprepared CASPs to immediately fund and execute formal, regulator-approved "wind-down" activities. Concurrently, the EBA’s codified methodology for fines transforms regulatory non-compliance from a reputational risk into a highly quantifiable, severe balance-sheet liability. (Note: Australia’s ASIC extension provides a vital, temporary safe harbour for APAC operations while firms prioritise EU compliance).

**5. Spiking Operational and Tech Resilience Costs (FinCrime & Cyber)**

The baseline operational expenditure (OPEX) required to run a compliant CASP is surging due to synchronised global mandates on technology risk and continuous financial crime monitoring.

- **Key Catalysts:** The EU’s AMLA guidelines on ongoing monitoring; UK MLRs 2026 updates; FATF cross-border payment transparency guidance; the EU’s DORA 2025 ICT incident reporting rules; Singapore’s MAS amendments to Technology Risk Management.

- **Materiality:** Compliance is shifting from static KYC to expensive, continuous, AI-driven behavioural monitoring and Travel Rule enforcement. Simultaneously, updates to DORA (EU) and MAS (Singapore) transform IT disruptions and third-party vendor management into board-level regulatory breaches. Technology budgets must be aggressively expanded to meet these bank-grade cybersecurity standards, which will heavily compress margins on low-volume retail trading.

**Strategic Imperatives for the C-Suite**

1. **Execute EU/MiCA Decision Matrix:** Formally document and fund MiCA transition plans or initiate ESMA-compliant wind-down procedures immediately to avoid EBA-calculated punitive fines.

2. **Audit Global Liquidity Architecture:** Assess reliance on global shared order books and unbacked stablecoins. Model the financial impact of ring-fencing operations in strict jurisdictions (HK, US) and prepare for the strategic delisting of non-compliant stablecoin pairs.

3. **Re-evaluate Derivatives Exposure:** Conduct a legal gap analysis on all derivative and DeFi offerings against proposed SEC/CFTC swap definitions to prevent sudden enforcement actions in US-adjacent markets.

4. **Recalibrate OPEX Budgets:** Divert capital from aggressive market expansion to RegTech, prioritising DORA/MAS-compliant ICT infrastructure and FATF-compliant continuous cross-border transaction monitoring capabilities.

## Europe

### MiCA:ESMA statement on MiCA transitional period and how to wind down activities

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** European Union / ESMA

**Source:** [esma.europa.eu](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-06/ESMA75-113276571-1710_Public_Statement_MiCA_transitional_period_ends.pdf)

**Summary:** ESMA has published explicit expectations for unauthorised Crypto Asset Service Providers (CASPs) regarding the mandatory wind-down of EU activities following the expiration of the MiCA transitional period. The regulator mandates an immediate cessation of client onboarding and marketing, restricting services strictly to asset reallocation and position closures.

**What it means for firms:** Unauthorised CASPs lose all market access and the ability to generate new business within the EU. Firms must maintain rigorous AML/CFT transaction monitoring during the exit phase, ensuring the secure and transparent return of client assets.

**Next Steps:** Management must immediately halt all EU-targeted marketing and turn off regional onboarding gateways. Firms are required to execute a documented wind-down plan and issue clear communications to EU clients outlining automated closure deadlines.

**Timeline:** Immediate operational effect.

**Assessment:**

**Materiality:** Total cessation of revenue-generating trading and custody services for unauthorised entities in the EU. Firms must temporarily pivot their operational architecture purely to support liquidity extraction and asset return without earning traditional fee revenue.

### MiCA:EBA consults on methodology for setting fines under MiCA

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** European Union / EBA

**Source:** [eba.europa.eu](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/european-banking-authority-consults-draft-methodology-setting-fines-under-markets-crypto-assets)

**Summary:** The EBA has launched a consultation on a two-step methodology for imposing fines on issuers of significant asset-referenced tokens (ARTs) and e-money tokens (EMTs) that breach MiCA. The methodology involves calculating a basic fine amount and applying adjustments based on specific aggravating or mitigating factors.

**What it means for firms:** Token issuers face a formalised, highly structured punitive regime modelled on existing EU financial regulatory frameworks. While the predictability of fine calculations increases, the regulatory exposure for non-compliance with reserve or transparency rules is severe.

**Next Steps:** Legal and compliance teams at major token issuers should review the consultation to understand the specific vectors used to calculate fines. Firms may submit feedback on the methodology ahead of the September deadline.

**Timeline:** Public hearing scheduled for 16th July 2026; consultation closes 28th September 2026.

**Assessment:**

**Materiality:** While this introduces no direct structural prohibition, it fundamentally alters the risk calculus of issuing significant tokens in the EU. A failure in operational resilience or reserve management now carries an explicit threat of crippling regulatory fines.

### FinCrime:AMLA consults on draft guidelines on ongoing monitoring of business relationships under AML Regulation

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** EU / AMLA

**Source:** [amla.europa.eu](https://www.amla.europa.eu/amla-consults-draft-guidelines-ongoing-monitoring-business-relationships_en)

**Summary:** The EU Anti-Money Laundering Authority (AMLA) launched a consultation on draft guidelines regarding the ongoing monitoring of business relationships under the AMLR. The guidelines mandate highly prescriptive frameworks for event-driven Customer Due Diligence (CDD) reviews and establish strict protocols for designing and testing transaction monitoring systems.

**What it means for firms:** EU-operating CASPs will be required to transition from static or periodic KYC refresh cycles to sophisticated, automated, and dynamic risk profiling. AMLA’s prescriptive standards require highly tested transaction monitoring tools capable of triggering real-time CDD updates based on on-chain activities and behavioural shifts.

**Next Steps:** Compliance and engineering teams must map their current transaction monitoring parameters and event-driven CDD review triggers against AMLA’s proposed guidelines. Firms must evaluate whether their underlying RegTech platforms can handle the required high-velocity, continuous blockchain monitoring requirements.

**Timeline:** The deadline for responses is 3rd September 2026. Final guidelines expected Q4 2026.

**Assessment:**

**Materiality:** Changes required. The compliance operating model must shift to continuous lifecycle management, requiring highly responsive, automated anomaly detection tightly integrated with the ongoing CDD workflow.

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** EU / ESAs (EBA, EIOPA, ESMA)

**Source:** [eba.europa.eu](https://www.eba.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-06/29b60c21-4ff3-4e1e-9308-7c8225d5cc01/ESAs%202025%20report%20on%20major%20ICT-related%20incidents.pdf)

**Summary:** The European Supervisory Authorities (ESAs) published a joint report detailing major ICT-related incidents under the Digital Operational Resilience Act (DORA) during 2025. The report highlights that nearly a third of major incidents stemmed from failures attributable to third-party providers, underscoring systemic vulnerabilities in shared infrastructure.

**What it means for firms:** As MiCA-regulated CASPs fall under DORA, this signals a high likelihood of aggressive supervisory scrutiny on Third-Party Risk Management (TPRM). Firms heavily reliant on outsourced cloud providers, blockchain node infrastructure, or shared platforms face strict liability for vendor failures.

**Next Steps:** Risk and IT Procurement teams must urgently review all critical third-party ICT service contracts to ensure full compliance with DORA standards. Executives must demand board-level reporting on vendor vulnerability management and cross-border incident containment strategies.

**Timeline:** Published on 3rd June 2026. DORA is actively in force, with immediate supervisory scrutiny applicable.

**Assessment:**

**Materiality:** Changes required. Heavy reliance on centralised or concentrated third-party ICT providers without rigorous oversight and redundant multi-vendor fallback strategies presents a severe operational compliance risk.

## United Kingdom

### Digital Assets:BoE policy statement on regulating systemic stablecoins

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** United Kingdom / Bank of England (BoE)

**Source:** [bankofengland.co.uk](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2026/june/boe-launches-policy-statement-and-draft-rules-on-regulating-systemic-stablecoins)

**Summary:** The Bank of England has finalised its regulatory approach for sterling-denominated systemic stablecoins and published a draft Code of Practice. The regime imposes temporary guardrails on maximum issuance levels per product to mitigate credit-provision risks, alongside stringent rules on reserve assets and capital.

**What it means for firms:** Issuers face an immediate artificial ceiling on growth due to the issuance guardrails, directly limiting market penetration and scale. Furthermore, the dual-regulatory structure (FCA and BoE) will impose rigorous operational requirements for reserve safeguarding and capital buffers.

**Next Steps:** Stablecoin issuers must review the draft Code of Practice and prepare for dual supervisory oversight. Firms should model their reserve-asset structures against historical liquidity stress events to ensure compliance with the newly adjusted backing requirements.

**Timeline:** Consultation on the draft Code of Practice closes 22nd September 2026, with final implementation expected by the end of 2026.

**Assessment:**

**Materiality:** The temporary guardrail fundamentally caps the scalability of new systemic stablecoins, restricting network effects until the BoE lifts the limits. Issuers must redesign their treasury management strategies to comply with highly restrictive reserve asset rules.

### FinCrime:Economic sanctions: OFSI and OFAC jointly publish comparative overview of US and UK regimes

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** United Kingdom & United States / OFSI & OFAC

**Source:** [ofsi.blog.gov.uk](https://ofsi.blog.gov.uk/2026/06/23/achieving-our-objectives-supporting-our-stakeholders-ofac-ofsi-enhanced-partnership-exchange-2026/)

**Summary:** The UK’s OFSI and the US’s OFAC have released a comparative overview of their respective economic sanctions frameworks. The guidance highlights critical divergences, notably that the UK considers entities “controlled” by designated persons to be sanctioned and does not apply the US 50% ownership threshold on an aggregate basis.

**What it means for firms:** Global CASPs operating across the US and UK must decouple their automated sanctions screening logic, as a unified approach will lead to compliance failures. The UK’s inclusion of broad “control” thresholds requires far deeper Ultimate Beneficial Ownership (UBO) unravelling than standard OFAC checks.

**Next Steps:** Legal and risk teams must update corporate screening policies to reflect the bifurcated US/UK definitions of ownership and control. Automated KYB (Know Your Business) systems and blockchain analytics tools must be recalibrated to run separate rulesets for OFAC and OFSI compliance.

**Timeline:** Immediate operational relevance; strict liability standards apply in both jurisdictions.

**Assessment:**

**Materiality:** High risk of catastrophic compliance failures if screening engines apply monolithic, US-centric aggregation rules to UK operations. Institutional onboarding will require longer lead times to satisfy the UK’s subjective “control” assessments.

**What it means for firms:** VASPs facilitating cross-border crypto transfers will need to adapt to stricter interpretations regarding the transmission of originator and beneficiary data. **Timeline:** Consultation responses are due by 21st August 2026; the global implementation target is the end of 2030.

**Assessment:**

**Materiality:** Imposes structural changes on how peer-to-peer and cross-border transactions are processed, increasing latency and operational friction. Failure to adapt screening protocols for new wallet types risks regulatory censure and loss of counterparty banking relationships.

### FinCrime:Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (Amendment) Regulations 2026

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** UK / HM Treasury

**Source:** [statutoryinstruments.parliament.uk](https://statutoryinstruments.parliament.uk/instrument/8cBIMh8j)

**Summary:** The UK has published the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing (Amendment) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/621), which enact statutory changes to strengthen the AML/CFT framework for digital assets significantly. The legislation introduces mandatory Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) for cryptoasset correspondent relationships, updates the definition of high-risk third countries to align directly with FATF standards, and amends the change-in-control regime for registered cryptoasset businesses.

**What it means for firms:** CASPs facilitating cross-border B2B liquidity or maintaining institutional relationships will face substantially higher due diligence and continuous monitoring burdens when operating in or interacting with the UK. Additionally, the amended change-in-control regime introduces immediate regulatory friction for corporate M&A, demanding extended FCA notifications, approvals, and scrutiny before executing equity adjustments involving registered crypto entities.

**Next Steps:** Compliance teams must immediately conduct a gap analysis of their institutional B2B onboarding procedures to ensure readiness for the new correspondent EDD standards and the updated high-risk country definitions. Concurrently, the Corporate Development and Legal teams must formally integrate the modified change-in-control stipulations into execution timelines and regulatory risk assessments for any active or planned M&A transactions.

**Timeline:** General provisions and high-risk third-country screening updates come into force on 30th June 2026. The specific new EDD requirements for cryptoasset correspondent relationships take effect on 1st February 2027.

**Assessment:**

**Materiality:** Changes required. CASPs that offer institutional liquidity, clearing, or correspondent services must fundamentally redesign their counterparty risk assessment processes to meet the elevated EDD thresholds. Furthermore, any strategic M&A or capital restructuring involving UK-registered CASPs will face stricter regulatory vetting, structurally reducing agility in corporate transactions.

### Risk:Stablecoin remuneration on centralised exchanges

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** Global Standards / Bank for International Settlements (BIS)

**Source:** [bis.org](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull125.htm)

**Summary:** On 19th June 2026, the BIS published a bulletin detailing how centralised exchanges generate yields for stablecoin holders through either “reserve-based” or “activity-based” models. The reserve-based model passes on returns from the stablecoin issuer’s safe reserve assets—such as Treasury bills and bank deposits—which closely track monetary policy rates. Conversely, the activity-based model funds yield by deploying customer stablecoins into riskier intermediation activities such as collateralised lending, margin finance, and trading, resulting in highly volatile yields driven by crypto market demand. The BIS notes that these differing remuneration models expose holders to distinct risks and could trigger macro-financial instability, such as redemption runs, if stablecoins see widespread adoption.

**What it means for firms:** This publication highlights a growing regulatory focus on how CASPs handle customer funds in “earn” or reward programs. Exchanges utilising activity-based models face elevated scrutiny regarding counterparty risk, particularly since traditional securities intermediaries are required to fully segregate client funds rather than use them for their own activities. If reserve-based stablecoins begin to substitute for traditional bank deposits aggressively, CASPs should anticipate stricter capital and liquidity regulations designed to protect the broader financial system and preserve the transmission of monetary policy.

**Next Steps:** Risk and Product teams must urgently audit current stablecoin yield offerings to classify them strictly as activity-based or reserve-based. Furthermore, firms must evaluate whether their current user disclosures accurately reflect the specific risks—such as counterparty exposure or market volatility—inherent to the model they employ.

**Timeline: 19th June 2026:** BIS Bulletin No. 125 published.

- **Immediate:** Internal review of stablecoin yield mechanisms and user disclosures.

**Assessment:**

**Materiality:** High. The explicit comparison between exchange practices and traditional securities intermediaries suggests future regulatory pressure to fully segregate client funds, which would severely disrupt the viability of activity-based yield products.

## Executive Summary

January 2026 marked a decisive turning point in global crypto regulation, driving a “Great Bifurcation” in CASP business models. We are witnessing a simultaneous **contraction of high-margin retail revenue** in key markets (UK, Japan) and a **massive expansion of institutional utility** in Europe. The era of the “borderless, agnostic” crypto exchange is effectively over; successful models must now specialise by jurisdiction—functioning as restricted broker-dealers in Asia/UK while pivoting to institutional liquidity provision in the EU.

**2. Critical Business Model Impacts**

**A. Revenue Disruption: The UK “Credit Ban” & Japan Reclassification**

- **UK “Phase 2” Threat (High Negative Impact):** The FCA’s Consultation (CP26/4) proposing a **ban on crypto credit and lending products** strikes at the heart of the “Retail Earn” economy. CASPs relying on net interest margin (NIM) or yield products for UK growth face immediate revenue attrition.

  - *Strategic Implication:* Firms must immediately decouple lending engines from UK interfaces to preserve their broader operating licenses for the **September 2026 Gateway**.

- **Japan’s Structural Shift:** The FSA’s move to transfer crypto assets from the Payment Services Act (PSA) to the **Financial Instruments and Exchange Act (FIEA)** redefines CASPs as financial instrument dealers.

  - *Strategic Implication:* This eliminates “payment service” regulatory arbitrage. Japan-facing units must restructure as securities brokerages, absorbing higher compliance costs but gaining access to derivatives and ETF distribution.

**B. Institutional Utility: The Eurosystem Liquidity Pivot**

- **Eurosystem DLT Collateral (High Positive Impact):** The Eurosystem’s decision to accept **DLT assets as collateral starting in March 2026** is a watershed moment for B2B models.

  - *Strategic Implication:* This validates high-quality digital assets as bankable High-Quality Liquid Assets (HQLA). CASPs with custody infrastructure should immediately pivot sales strategies to target Tier 1 banks, transforming custody from a storage cost centre into a high-value liquidity gateway.

**C. Market Access & Cost Base: The End of “Offshore” Viability**

- **UK Location Policy:** The FCA’s confirmation of a strict **Location Policy** effectively ends the offshore servicing model for the UK market.

  - *Cost Impact:* To access the September 2026 authorisation gateway, firms must incur significant CAPEX to capitalise and staff physical local subsidiaries.

- **Global Capital Efficiency:** With **Canada (OSFI)** and global regulators finalising capital treatment for crypto exposures (and **ESMA** tightening competence rules), the cost of holding crypto on-balance-sheet is rising.

  - *Cost Impact:* This incentivises a shift toward “agency” (non-principal) trading models to minimise capital deduction requirements.

**D. Operational Liability: The Shift to “Firm-Led” Risk**

- **Dubai (DFSA) Liability Transfer:** By abolishing the “Recognised List” in favour of **Firm-Led Token Assessment**, the DFSA has increased listing speed but transferred 100% of the due diligence liability to CASPs.

  - *Risk Impact:* Business models that depend on aggressive token listings now face heightened legal risk; internal listing committees must be strengthened to prevent enforcement blowback.

**3. US & Global Outlook: Harmonisation**

- **US Market Re-Entry:** The **SEC-CFTC “Project Crypto”** harmonisation and the Senate’s advancement of the **Digital Commodity Intermediaries Act** signal a stabilising US market structure. The window for regulatory arbitrage is closing, and a clear path for “Digital Commodity” registration is emerging.

- **Operational Resilience:** With the **UK Treasury Committee** demanding AI be designated a “Critical Third Party” and the **G7 roadmap for Post-Quantum Cryptography**, technical debt is becoming a compliance breach. Investment in quantum-safe encryption and AI governance is now a “license to operate” requirement.

**4. Recommendation**

Management should prioritise a **Q1 2026 Strategic Review** to:

1. **Defend:** Model the P&L impact of removing lending products from the UK market and prepare the local entity for the September 2026 Gateway.

2. **Attack:** Finalise technical readiness for Eurosystem collateral eligibility to capture institutional flows in March.

3. **Optimise:** Review balance sheet exposures against new Canadian/Basel capital treatment standards.

### OpRes:G7 Cyber Expert Group Roadmap for Transition to Post-Quantum Cryptography

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** International / UK (HM Treasury)

**Source:** [gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/advancing-a-coordinated-roadmap-for-the-transition-to-post-quantum-cryptography-in-the-financial-sector/g7-cyber-expert-group-statement-on-advancing-a-coordinated-roadmap-for-the-transition-to-post-quantum-cryptography-in-the-financial-sector-january-20)

**Summary:**

On 13 January 2026, HM Treasury published the G7 Cyber Expert Group’s roadmap for the financial sector’s transition to Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC). The statement warns that quantum computers could break current cryptographic protocols (the foundation of DLT and wallet security). It establishes a migration target of 2035, with immediate “discovery and inventory” phases required.

**What it means for firms:**

This is an existential strategic risk for the crypto industry. Unlike traditional banks that use cryptography for messaging, CASPs use it for asset ownership (private keys). The roadmap implies that current blockchain signing algorithms (e.g., ECDSA) will become non-compliant or uninsurable well before the 2035 deadline due to “Harvest Now, Decrypt Later” threats.

**Next Steps:**

Initiate a “Cryptographic Agility” assessment to inventory where quantum-vulnerable algorithms are used in custody, consensus, and transaction signing.

**Timeline:**

Roadmap released 13 Jan 2026; Planning target 2035.

**Assessment:**

Business Model Risk: Critical (Long-term). Protocols that cannot be upgraded may be deemed systemically unsafe by regulators.

### RegArchitecture: EBA Publishes Final Guidelines on “Ancillary Services Undertakings” (ASU)

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** EU / European Banking Authority (EBA)

**Source:** [eba.europa.eu](https://www.eba.europa.eu/activities/single-rulebook/regulatory-activities/accounting-and-auditing/guidelines-ancillary-services-undertakings)

**Summary:**

On January 9, 2026, the EBA finalised guidelines regarding “Ancillary Services Undertakings.” The guidelines clarify that “fintech and technology-driven services” provided by subsidiaries of banking groups must be identified as ancillary services. This mandates prudential consolidation, meaning the risks of the crypto/fintech subsidiary must be backed by the parent bank’s capital.

**What it means for firms:**

Bank-owned CASPs, or those in joint ventures with EU banks, may face stricter capital controls and reporting requirements. It eliminates the “regulatory arbitrage” of keeping high-risk crypto activities in a lightly capitalised subsidiary.

**Next Steps:**

Review corporate structure and capital adequacy at the group level.

**Timeline:**

Immediate application upon translation (Standard ~2 months).

**Assessment:**

Business Model Risk: Medium. May deter banks from aggressive crypto expansion or force the restructuring of BaaS (Banking-as-a-Service) relationships.

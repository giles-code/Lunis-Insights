## Executive Summary

This edition covers the review window 2-29 August 2026. Every item below was re-checked against primary regulatory sources on 31 August 2026; the verification note at the end of the report records the few corrections made and the one item that remains unverified.

August was the month the United States stopped waiting for Congress. Four federal authorities - the SEC, the Treasury, the CFTC and the OCC - each advanced a load-bearing piece of a crypto market-structure and stablecoin regime by rulemaking, order or charter rather than by statute. At the same time, the CLARITY Act sat parked ahead of a Senate cloture vote reported for 15 September. The SEC proposed Regulation Crypto Assets, a tailored offering framework with a conditional safe harbour and state-law preemption. The Treasury proposed the GENIUS Act section 3 definitions that determine which stablecoins a distributor may make available to US persons, against statutory deadlines of 18 January 2027 and 18 July 2028 that are already fixed. The OCC confirmed a repeatable federal route for issuer-custodians by conditionally approving the World Liberty Trust Company charter. The CFTC held the line on federal pre-emption for prediction markets by emergency order, and signalled a purpose-built “crypto asset market” designation if legislation stalls.

Outside the United States, the month was one of groundwork rather than new rules. The United Kingdom produced no new cryptoasset rule but published dense supervisory material - final transaction-reporting rules, a hard line on Annex 1 firms relying on parent-company financial crime controls, and high-growth-firm findings - that together read as the standard the FCA will apply when the cryptoasset authorisation gateway opens on 30 September. HM Treasury announced a new secondary Bank of England objective covering systemic stablecoins. The European Union was quiet on crypto-specific measures, with activity concentrated in EMIR and prudential workstreams and the MiCA review consultation now closing on 30 September.

Asia produced the month’s only supervisory expectations addressed directly to virtual asset service providers: Hong Kong’s SFC on the risks of taking client deposits through simplified eDDA arrangements, and Thailand’s SEC and central bank on stablecoin supervision, with Travel Rule regulations due to take effect in February 2027 - currently the firmest Travel Rule commencement date on the Asia calendar. Internationally, the BIS set out where standard-setters may push next, finding that stablecoin activity restrictions bind the issuing entity rather than the wider group, and its General Manager argued at Jackson Hole that tokenised deposits, not stablecoins, are the sounder path.

September is the densest month of the year. The Senate cloture vote (15 September), the opening of the FCA gateway and the close of both Bank of England stablecoin consultations and the extended MiCA review consultation (22 and 30 September) all land within five weeks of this report. The horizon scan at the end of this report sets out the full calendar.

## Europe

### EMIR workstreams: initial margin relief, SIMM reporting and third-country CCP reporting \[Medium\]

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** European Union - European Supervisory Authorities (EBA, EIOPA, ESMA)

**Publication date:** 3, 5 and 18 August 2026

🔗 **Source:** [ESAs press release on bilateral margin requirements, 3 Aug 2026](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-eiopa-and-esma-propose-amendments-bilateral-margin-requirements) \| [ESMA consultation on third-country CCP reporting, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-consults-reporting-framework-clearing-activity-recognised-third-country)

**Summary:** The European Supervisory Authorities submitted final draft regulatory technical standards on 3 August relieving counterparties below the EUR 8 billion threshold from exchanging initial margin on new and existing uncleared OTC derivative contracts. The EBA followed on 5 August with a consultation on a recurring reporting, validation and monitoring framework for users of the ISDA Standard Initial Margin Model, closing 2 November 2026, with a first reporting reference date expected in December 2027. ESMA consulted on 18 August on annual reporting of clearing activity at recognised third-country CCPs under EMIR 3, closing 12 October 2026.

**What it means for firms:** Relief is available for administrative purposes, and applicability turns on group structure rather than on cryptoasset activity. The first two items should be assessed together: an entity that falls below the threshold and ceases to exchange initial margin would not need SIMM validation, so the sequencing decision is worth taking once rather than twice.

**Timeline:** Final draft RTS submitted 3 August 2026. EBA consultation closes 2 November 2026, first reporting reference date expected December 2027. ESMA consultation closes 12 October 2026.

**Assessment:**

**Business model risk -** Low. No cryptoasset perimeter effect; exposure is limited to group entities transacting uncleared OTC derivatives.

### EBA prudential consultations: investment-firm reclassification and operational risk \[Low\]

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** European Union - European Banking Authority

**Publication date:** 25 and 26 August 2026

🔗 **Source:** [EBA consultation on operational risk management RTS, 26 Aug 2026](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-consults-draft-technical-standards-institutions-operational-risk-management) \| [EBA press releases index](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases)

**Summary:** The EBA opened two prudential consultations relevant to group banking and MiFID entities rather than to cryptoasset activity: revised technical standards on the reclassification of investment firms as credit institutions under the EUR 30 billion threshold, published 25 August and closing 25 November 2026, covering the methodology at both solo and group level; and draft technical standards on operational risk management under Article 323 of the Capital Requirements Regulation, published 26 August and closing 31 December 2026.

**What it means for firms:** No cryptoasset perimeter effect. Relevant only where a group operates an EU investment firm approaching the reclassification threshold, in which case the methodology at solo and consolidated level is the item to read, because reclassification brings the full credit-institution prudential regime with it.

**Timeline:** Consultations close 25 November 2026 and 31 December 2026 respectively.

**Assessment:**

**Business model risk -** Low, unless an EU investment firm in the group is near the threshold, in which case the risk is a step change in prudential treatment rather than an incremental one.

### ECB: offline digital euro standards, and 0.2% crypto acceptance among online sellers \[Low\]

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** European Union - European Central Bank

**Publication date:** 13 and 18 August 2026

🔗 **Source:** [ECB call for expression of interest, offline digital euro, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/intro/news/html/ecb.mipnews260818.en.html) \| [ECB study on the use of cash by companies, August 2026](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/use-of-cash/html/ecb.uccea202608.en.html)

**Timeline:** Call for expression of interest closes 25 September 2026—no implementation date for the offline digital euro.

**Assessment:**

**Business model risk -** Low now, strategic later: a widely distributed offline digital euro would compete directly with euro stablecoin payment use cases.

## United Kingdom

### FCA PS26/15: final rules replacing the UK MiFIR transaction reporting regime \[Medium\]

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** United Kingdom - Financial Conduct Authority (PS26/15)

**Publication date:** 3 August 2026

🔗 **Source:** [FCA PS26/15](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/policy-statements/ps26-15-improving-uk-transaction-reporting-regime) \| [FCA press release on transaction reporting savings](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-finalises-rules-cut-firms-transaction-reporting-costs-over-100m-year)

**Summary:** The FCA published final rules on 3 August replacing the UK MiFIR transaction reporting regime. Reportable fields fall from 65 to 52; roughly seven million instruments traded only on EU venues come out of scope, as do FX derivatives, affecting more than 400 firms; back-reporting is shortened from five years to three. The FCA estimates a net annual saving to industry of GBP 108 million, taking the annual cost from GBP 493 million to around GBP 385 million. A consultation on the reporting schema, validation rules and transitional provisions follows in October 2026.

**What it means for firms:** Not crypto-specific, but a group’s UK MiFID entity sits inside the perimeter. The October schema consultation, not this policy statement, is the gating item for any reporting-logic rebuild, and the long runway to April 2028 should not be read as a licence to defer scoping until the schema is settled.

**Timeline:** Published 3 August 2026; schema consultation October 2026; rules in force 3 April 2028, with a flexible supervisory approach from 3 August 2026 to 2 April 2028.

**Assessment:**

**Business model risk -** Low and manageable, provided the rebuild is sequenced off the October schema rather than off the policy statement.

### FCA applies increased scrutiny to Annex 1 firms and to reliance on group controls \[Medium\]

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** United Kingdom - Financial Conduct Authority

**Publication date:** 7 August 2026 (updated 13 August 2026)

🔗 **Source:** [FCA statement on increased scrutiny of Annex 1 firms](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/statements/fca-applying-increased-scrutiny-annex-1-firms)

**Summary:** The FCA published a statement on 7 August setting out increased scrutiny of Annex 1 financial institutions. It warns specifically that firms cannot rely on off-the-shelf group or parent-company financial crime procedures and that each firm must assess its suitability for its own business, tells applicants to expect registration to take longer, and records an information request sent to around 900 Annex 1 firms, building on earlier work with 300 firms in late 2025.

**What it means for firms:** The central criticism describes the standard operating model of a UK subsidiary in a global group: financial crime policy written at parent level and adopted locally. The same challenge should be expected at the cryptoasset authorisation gateway, so UK-specific evidence of local risk assessment, tailoring and testing should be assembled before an application is filed rather than produced in response to a case-officer question.

**Timeline:** Statement published 7 August 2026, updated 13 August 2026. Relevant to applications filed from the gateway opening on 30 September 2026.

**Assessment:**

**Business model risk -** Medium and directly relevant to the UK authorisation path: reliance on group controls is now an identified supervisory concern rather than an accepted norm.

## Hong Kong

### SFC circular on mitigating risks in simplified eDDA deposit arrangements \[Medium\]

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** Hong Kong - Securities and Futures Commission (ref. SFO/IS/033/2026; e-distribution ref. 26EC51)

**Publication date:** 20 August 2026 (applies from issue)

🔗 **Source:** [SFC circular: mitigating risks in receiving deposits through simplified eDDA arrangements](https://apps.sfc.hk/edistributionWeb/api/circular/openFile?lang=EN&refNo=26EC51)

**Summary:** The SFC issued a circular on 20 August to licensed corporations, SFC-licensed virtual asset service providers and associated entities on the risks of receiving client deposits through simplified electronic Direct Debit Authorisation arrangements, under which a client sets up an FPS direct debit authorisation inside the firm’s own trading app or website, removing a bank-side control point. The SFC identifies impersonation and unauthorised-access risk, and financial risk arising from the indemnity the firm provides to the payee bank.

Expected measures include a pre-implementation review of the indemnity and contractual terms and of the firm’s capacity to absorb the risk; verification of account ownership before processing set-up requests, including small-value test transfers; ongoing red-flag monitoring for repeated failed set-up attempts, large unexplained deposits and activity inconsistent with the client profile, with reporting to the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit; mitigation through transaction limits or frequency caps, withholding periods after deposits, or step-up authentication; and disclosure to clients of their eDDA settings.

**What it means for firms:** The only supervisory circular of the month addressed to the cryptoasset licence category by name, and immediately actionable without waiting for a rulemaking to conclude. The control set is jurisdiction-neutral and should be assessed against every instant-deposit rail operated, not only Hong Kong’s FPS. The indemnity analysis is a balance-sheet question, because in a simplified arrangement the firm rather than the bank generally carries the loss on an unauthorised set-up. The practical inspection test is whether the pre-implementation assessment and the red-flag monitoring can be evidenced.

**Timeline:** Issued 20 August 2026; supervisory expectations apply from issue, with no transitional period.

**Assessment:**

**Business model risk -** Moderate and immediate for any SFC-licensed group entity offering simplified eDDA, with a live read-across risk to instant-deposit rails in other jurisdictions.

## Thailand

### SEC and Bank of Thailand on stablecoin supervision and the Travel Rule \[Medium\]

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** Thailand - Securities and Exchange Commission, jointly with the Bank of Thailand (News No. 165/2026)

**Publication date:** 7 August 2026

🔗 **Source:** [Thai SEC News No. 165/2026](https://www.sec.or.th/EN/Pages/News_Detail.aspx?SECID=13185&NewsNo=165&NewsYear=2026&Lang=EN)

**Summary:** The SEC and the Bank of Thailand met digital asset business operators on 7 August to discuss supervisory approaches to stablecoin transactions. The approaches discussed were enhanced customer identification and due diligence; customer profiling used to set daily withdrawal limits; Travel Rule preparation; “smart detection” of suspicious transaction patterns; and screening guidelines for mule accounts and links to high-risk or watchlisted wallets, with monitoring focused on stablecoin transactions such as USDT and USDC. The release states that Travel Rule regulations are expected to be issued by August 2026 and to take effect in February 2027.

**What it means for firms:** A dated near-term compliance obligation rather than a policy signal, and currently the firmest Travel Rule commencement date on the Asia calendar. Profiling-based withdrawal caps are a product constraint that has to be built into the limits engine rather than bolted onto surveillance. Travel Rule implementation requires counterparty VASP discovery and a messaging-protocol decision, with lead times realistically longer than the runway between issuance and effect. The joint SEC and central bank framing groups payments-side and securities-side treatment, which reduces the scope for arbitrage between the two perimeters.

**Timeline:** Meeting reported 7 August 2026; Travel Rule regulations expected by August 2026 per the release; effective February 2027.

**Assessment:**

**Business model risk -** Moderate and operational for any group entity licensed in Thailand or serving Thai residents, and a template for ASEAN Travel Rule direction more widely. A firm without a solution before February 2027 faces a hard stop on non-compliant transfers.

## United States

### SEC proposes Regulation Crypto Assets \[High\]

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** United States - Securities and Exchange Commission (Release 33-11434; paired release 34-106150; File No. S7-2026-27)

**Publication date:** 18 August 2026; Federal Register 21 August 2026 (verified)

🔗 **Source:** [SEC press release 2026-76](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2026-76-sec-proposes-new-regulation-crypto-assets) \| [Proposed rule 33-11434 (PDF)](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/proposed/2026/33-11434.pdf) \| [Federal Register, 21 Aug 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/21/2026-17183/regulation-crypto-assets)

**Summary:** The Commission proposed a tailored securities offering framework for certain investment contracts involving crypto assets, following its March 2026 interpretive release. It comprises a one-time startup exemption permitting offerings of up to USD 5 million over four years; a two-tier fundraising exemption modelled on Regulation A permitting up to USD 75 million in every 12 months, with financial-condition disclosure, audited financial statements at certain thresholds and ongoing reporting; a conditional safe harbour under which a crypto asset is deemed no longer subject to an investment contract where the issuer certifies that it has ceased or terminated all essential managerial efforts it promised to undertake and satisfies other conditions; and preemption of state securities registration and qualification requirements, achieved through a qualified purchaser definition, for covered offers, sales and certain secondary market transactions. Antifraud and antimanipulation provisions continue to apply.

An open meeting noticed for 14 August was cancelled on 13 August and the proposal issued without one. Three Commissioner statements were published alongside the proposal, all supportive; no dissent had been published as at month-end, which bears on the proposal’s durability.

**What it means for firms:** The most significant development for ecosystem and platform features - launchpads, launchpools, token generation events and airdrops - since the regime question arose, and it materially changes the US listing perimeter for spot trading. The safe harbour gives a listing committee a certifiable end-state for a token’s security status, and state preemption removes the blue-sky overlay that has made US token distribution impractical at scale. Read with the Treasury and CFTC items, three agencies are building a market-structure regime by rule rather than waiting for statute. The risk is premature reliance: an issuer that self-assesses into the safe harbour before final rules, or a venue that lists on that basis, would be exposed if conditions tighten between proposal and adoption.

**Timeline:** Proposed 18 August 2026; Federal Register 21 August 2026; comments due 20 October 2026 (verified against the Federal Register notice). No adoption date.

**Assessment:**

**Business model risk -** Opportunity-led. If adopted broadly as proposed, US token distribution becomes viable inside a defined perimeter, and a listing framework built on the assumption that US primary distribution is closed will be out of date.

### Treasury proposes GENIUS Act section 3 rules on stablecoin issuance, offer and sale \[High\]

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** United States - Department of the Treasury

**Publication date:** 17 August 2026; Federal Register 18 August 2026 (verified)

🔗 **Source:** [Treasury release SB0605](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0605) \| [Federal Register, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/18/2026-16796/genius-act-regulations-on-payment-stablecoin-issuance-offer-and-sale)

**Summary:** The Treasury proposed rules implementing section 3 of the GENIUS Act, defining what it means to issue a payment stablecoin in the United States - which determines when an issuer needs a GENIUS licence - and what it means to offer or sell a payment stablecoin to a person in the United States. Under the proposal, a person issues in the United States only if, at the time of issuance, that person is located in the United States or issues to a person located in the United States; the offer-or-sale definition captures conduct such as direct solicitation of, and advertising availability to, persons located in the United States.

Three statutory milestones frame the rule. From 18 January 2027, a person generally may not issue a payment stablecoin in the United States without an appropriate federal or state licence. Digital asset service providers generally may not make foreign-issued payment stablecoins available unless the foreign issuer has the technological capability to comply with, and will comply with, any lawful order and any reciprocal arrangement. From 18 July 2028, a digital asset service provider may not offer or sell a payment stablecoin to a person in the United States unless a permitted payment stablecoin issuer issued it.

**What it means for firms:** The most directly binding item of the month, because it regulates the firm as a digital asset service provider and not merely as a prospective issuer. It covers fiat and payment solutions, spot trading in any pair quoted against a foreign-issued stablecoin, and stablecoin yield. Two definitions determine everything: the extraterritorial reach of offering or selling to a person in the United States, and the foreign-issuer condition, which in practice is a freeze-and-seize capability assessment on every listed stablecoin. Unlike the SEC proposal, the underlying obligations are already in statute with fixed commencement dates, and the rulemaking settles only scope, so delisting decisions may be forced on a timetable the firm does not control.

**Timeline:** Proposed 17 August 2026; Federal Register 18 August 2026; comments due 19 October 2026 (verified). Statutory milestones: 18 January 2027 and 18 July 2028.

**Assessment:**

**Business model risk -** High and dated. A listed foreign-issued stablecoin whose issuer cannot meet the lawful-order condition becomes undistributable to US persons from 18 January 2027, and every listed payment stablecoin must trace to a licensed issuer from 18 July 2028.

### BIS: stablecoin activity restrictions bind the issuer, not the group \[Medium\]

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** International - Bank for International Settlements, Financial Stability Institute (FSI Brief No 33)

**Publication date:** 27 August 2026

🔗 **Source:** [FSI Brief No 33: Regulating stablecoin issuance - permissible entities and activities](https://www.bis.org/publications/fsi-brief-33-regulating-stablecoin-issuance--permissible-entities-and-activities)

**Summary:** The BIS Financial Stability Institute published FSI Brief No 33, “Regulating stablecoin issuance: permissible entities and activities”, by Adrien Currat, Johannes Ehrentraud and Denise Garcia Ocampo. Comparing stablecoin issuance regimes across the EU, Hong Kong, Singapore, the UK and the US, it finds that activity restrictions apply to the issuing entity rather than to the group, so that affiliates of a non-bank issuer may lend, stake or provide custody where the issuer itself may not; consolidated supervision covers banks but not non-bank issuers, so that restrictions can be circumvented through corporate structuring.

Any group design that relies on affiliate structures to conduct activities the issuer itself cannot should be stress-tested against a future group-level restriction rather than presented as a settled structure. This brief was carried as unverified in the weekly reporting because the conventional bis.org path does not resolve; it has now been located and verified at the publications URL above.

**Timeline:** Published 27 August 2026. No consultation or implementation timetable - it is an analytical brief, not a standard.

**Assessment:**

**Business model risk -** Low now, structural later. A group-level restriction would reach the affiliate activities that currently sit outside the issuer perimeter.

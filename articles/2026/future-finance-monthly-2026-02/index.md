## Executive Summary

February 2026 marks a definitive inflexion point across all major jurisdictions (US, EU, UK, and APAC). Regulators have transitioned from drafting frameworks to aggressive, data-driven enforcement. The era of regulatory arbitrage, offshore "reverse solicitation,” and asynchronous compliance is over. CASPs must now operate with the structural maturity, capital adequacy, and enterprise-grade tech stacks of traditional Tier-1 financial institutions.

### Top three material business model impacts

**1. The "De-Anonymisation and Financial Crime Clampdown**

Global regulators are systematically dismantling on-chain anonymity and forcing CASPs to act as heavily armed financial gatekeepers.

- **The Impact:** The US IRS has finalised Form 1099 reporting rules, requiring custodial brokers and DeFi interfaces to report gross proceeds—fundamentally altering the US user experience. Simultaneously, the EU’s AMLA is beginning data collection for direct supervision, and the US Treasury is pushing for mandatory AI-driven blockchain surveillance under the GENIUS Act.

- **Business Model Threat:** High. Firms can no longer rely on basic, manual KYC/AML. Massive capital expenditure (CAPEX) is required immediately to procure enterprise-grade, automated transaction monitoring, tax reporting infrastructure, and real-time Travel Rule enforcement.

**2. Severe Capital & Liquidity Squeezes vs New Product Unlocks**

Capital efficiency is under attack globally, though new, highly lucrative product avenues are opening for fully compliant firms.

- **The Squeeze:** The US Federal Reserve (via ISDA SIMM guidelines) is isolating crypto into a bespoke, high-risk class, meaning OTC desks will face significantly higher capital lock-ups for uncleared derivatives. In the EU, CASPs face an imminent "cliff edge" (March 2026) to align Electronic Money Token (EMT) flows with PSD2, threatening stablecoin payment revenues.

- **The Unlock:** Conversely, Hong Kong (SFC) has officially permitted margin financing and perpetual contracts for professional investors, and Japan is advancing a 20% flat tax alongside wholesale DLT integration.

- **Business Model Opportunity:** Accretive, but capital-intensive. Firms must immediately optimise their balance sheets to support margin-heavy derivative expansions in APAC while ring-fencing capital to meet strict new stablecoin and OTC requirements in the West.

**3. Cybersecurity Upgraded to Strict Regulatory Liability**

Cyber hygiene has crossed the Rubicon from an internal IT concern to a primary vector for severe regulatory enforcement and market exclusion.

- **The Impact:** Australia’s ASIC secured a landmark \$2.5M Federal Court penalty against a financial licensee purely for cybersecurity failures. Concurrently, the EU and the UK executed a binding MoU to supervise jointly and stress-test Critical ICT Third-Party Providers (CTPPs) under DORA.

- **Business Model Threat:** Critical. Relying on concentrated, unregulated third-party cloud or blockchain node vendors is now a direct breach of compliance. A cyber breach no longer just costs client funds; it triggers immediate, multi-million-dollar regulatory fines and potential license revocation.

## Europe

### RegArchitecture: ECON draft report on digital assets

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** EU / European Parliament (ECON Committee)

**Source:** [europarl.europa.eu](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/doceo/document/ECON-PR-784381_EN.pdf)

**Summary:** On 23 February 2026, the ECON Committee published a draft report addressing the challenges posed by digital assets under the MiCA Regulation and DLT Pilot Regime. The report calls for urgent legislation clarifying the multi-issuance of fungible stablecoins by EU and non-EU entities, strengthened AML/CFT measures to prevent evasion, and tighter alignment of rules for significant multi-function groups (MFGs).

**What it means for firms:** CASPs operating vertically integrated business models (MFGs) or issuing multi-jurisdictional stablecoins face a clear signal of tightening legislation. The European Parliament is actively scrutinising regulatory arbitrage, meaning firms will face heightened supervisory dialogue and expectations regarding on-chain AML/CFT transaction monitoring.

**Next Steps:** Strategy and public policy teams must monitor the progression of this draft resolution to anticipate targeted amendments to the MiCA framework. Global stablecoin issuers must review their cross-border issuance vehicles against the Parliament’s demand for stronger prudential safeguards.

**Timeline:** Draft report published 23 February 2026; subject to further legislative debate and adoption.

**Assessment:**

**Materiality:** Changes required. Entities issuing fungible stablecoins globally may need to fundamentally restructure their issuance and reserve segregation models to satisfy emerging EU rules on multi-issuance. Multi-function groups face pressure to unbundle services to align with tighter prudential oversight.

### Digital Assets: ESMA public statement on derivatives in scope of national product intervention measures on CFDs

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** EU / European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA)

**Source:** [esma.europa.eu](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-reminds-firms-their-obligations-under-cfd-product-intervention-measures)

**Summary:** On 24 February 2026, ESMA issued a public statement clarifying that derivatives providing leveraged exposure to cryptoassets (such as Bitcoin or Ethereum), often marketed as “perpetual futures,” fall within the scope of national product intervention measures on CFDs. ESMA warned that commercial naming is irrelevant and these products must comply with strict rules, including leverage limits, margin close-outs, negative balance protection, and PRIIPs disclosures.

**What it means for firms:** Crypto exchanges offering leveraged perpetual contracts to EU retail investors must immediately treat these products as highly regulated CFDs. Failure to apply strict operational constraints—specifically, leverage caps and negative balance protection—exposes firms to immediate enforcement actions for breaching existing product intervention measures.

**Next Steps:** Compliance and product teams must urgently audit their derivative offerings to EU retail clients. Any leveraged crypto derivative not physically settled must be mapped against CFD intervention measures, and non-compliant products must be geo-blocked or structurally reconfigured.

**Timeline:** Immediate application, as this is an explicit clarification of active enforcement regarding existing national CFD restrictions.

**Assessment:**

**Materiality:** Changes required. Platforms heavily reliant on high-leverage retail crypto derivatives must fundamentally restructure their margin engines to enforce strict caps and prevent negative balances, or restrict these products exclusively to professional clients.

**Assessment:**

**Materiality:** Changes required. Overreliance on outsourced, unregulated digital infrastructure now poses a direct compliance risk; enhanced due diligence and legally binding resilience SLAs with all tech vendors are mandatory.

### FinCrime: EBA and AMLA complete handover of AML/CFT mandates

**Jurisdiction / regulator:** EU / EBA & AMLA

**Source:** [eba.europa.eu](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-and-amla-complete-handover-amlcft-mandates)

**Summary:** On 19 January 2026, the European Banking Authority (EBA) officially concluded its standalone AML/CFT mandate, transferring central responsibilities, supervisory insights, and databases (such as EuReCa) to the new EU Anti-Money Laundering Authority (AMLA). AMLA is now the central authority tasked with finalising the EU’s Single Rulebook for financial crime and will directly supervise the bloc’s highest-risk financial institutions.

**What it means for firms:** This transition signals a unified and potentially more aggressive pan-European supervisory approach to financial crime, replacing fragmented national interpretations. Large, cross-border CASPs could eventually fall under AMLA’s direct centralised supervision, and all European digital asset operations will be subjected to strict, harmonised AML/CFT technical standards.

**Next Steps:** Compliance teams must immediately pivot their regulatory radar from the EBA to AMLA for all future guidance and regulatory technical standards. Firms should formally document that existing EBA AML/CFT guidelines remain fully in force and legally binding until AMLA issues explicit replacements.

**Timeline:** Handover completed internally on 1 January 2026 and officially announced on 19 January 2026. AMLA will directly supervise 40 high-risk financial entities starting in 2028.

**Assessment:**

**Materiality:** Changes required. Firms currently exploiting jurisdictional arbitrage regarding AML interpretations across EU member states will face a unified standard, necessitating a consolidated, pan-European financial crime framework.

## Executive Summary

December 2025 marked a definitive structural shift in the global crypto landscape from **regulatory containment to regulated integration**. For CASP business models, the “Wild West” era of arbitrage is effectively closed. The new operating reality is characterised by **expanded addressable markets** in the US, UK, and Japan, offset by **sharply rising capital and operational costs** in the EU and globally.

**Strategic Pivot:** Firms must transition from a low-capital, high-velocity exchange model to a **capital-intensive, infrastructure-grade service provider** model. This is necessary to survive incoming prudential regimes (Shadow Banking/Basel) while capturing new institutional revenue streams unlocked by banking and tax reforms.

**2. Material Impacts on Revenue Generation**

**A. Market Expansion & Liquidity (Japan & UK)**

- **Japan Tax Reform (High Revenue Impact):** The LDP’s approval of a **20% separate tax rate** for crypto assets (reclassifying them as financial products) removes the historic 55% tax barrier.

  - *Business Impact:* Anticipate a massive repatriation of retail and institutional capital in Japan—immediate **Action:** Prioritise marketing and liquidity provisioning in the APAC region to capture this surge in volume

- **UK Private Markets (PISCES & DSS):** The launch of **PISCES** and the **Digital Securities Sandbox (DSS)** creates a regulated venue for trading tokenised private shares.

  - *Business Impact:* Opens a new, non-cyclical revenue stream in **Real World Asset (RWA) tokenisation** and secondary market trading, allowing us to diversify revenue away from volatile crypto-spot markets.

**B. Banking Access & Cost of Funds (US & EU)**

- **US Banking Liberalisation:** The Federal Reserve’s rescission of its restrictive 2023 policy and the OCC’s granting of conditional Trust Charters effectively end “Operation Choke Point 2.0.”

  - *Business Impact:* Drastic reduction in payment processing churn and lower fees for fiat on/off-ramps. Firms can now re-establish Tier-1 banking partnerships to stabilise treasury operations.

- **EU Payment Systems Access:** The EU MoU, which allows non-bank PSPs to access central bank-operated payment systems, enables CASPs to bypass commercial banks.<sup>3</sup>

  - *Business Impact:* **Direct margin improvement.** By settling directly with the central bank layer, firms eliminate intermediary fees and settlement delays from commercial banks.

**3. Material Impacts on Business Model & Structure**

**A. The “Shadow Banking” Capital Shock (Global & UK)**

- **Prudential Regimes:** The **UK FCA’s consultation on a prudential regime** and the **FSB’s classification of crypto as “Shadow Banking”** indicate that regulators will demand higher capital buffers.

  - *Model Impact:* The “capital-light” agency model is obsolete. Firms must prepare for **lower Return on Equity (ROE)** as they are forced to ring-fence significant capital against operational risks and proprietary token holdings.

**B. Operational Compliance & Overhead (EU & Global)**

- **DORA & MiCA Costs:** The **“Shai-Hulud 2.0” (Supply Chain Integrity)** update under DORA and the new **MiCA XBRL mandate** require immediate CAPEX.

  - *Model Impact:* Compliance is no longer just a staffing cost but a heavy infrastructure cost. Vendor procurement cycles will lengthen significantly to meet supply chain integrity standards.

- **Global Tax Surveillance (Hong Kong):** Hong Kong’s implementation of the **Crypto-Asset Reporting Framework (CARF)** signals the international standard for automatic tax information exchange.

  - *Model Impact:* Client onboarding (KYC) taxonomies must be overhauled to capture granular tax residency data, potentially adding friction at the top of the funnel.

**C. Unbundling Risks (FSB)**

- **Multifunction Intermediaries:** The FSB report on **Multifunction Crypto-asset Intermediaries (MCIs)** suggests a global push to separate trading, custody, and lending functions.

  - *Model Impact:* Firms may be forced to structurally separate exchange arms from custody arms, increasing legal and operational overhead to avoid conflicts of interest.

4\. European Union

### RegArchitecture: European Commission adopts legislative proposals on integration of EU capital markets

Jurisdiction/Regulator: European Union / European Commission (ESMA)

**Source:** [finance.ec.europa.eu](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/publications/market-integration-package_en)

**Summary of Development:** On December 4, 2025, the European Commission proposed a legislative package to centralise supervision of all Crypto-Asset Service Providers (CASPs) under ESMA.2 This proposal eliminates the “significant entities” model to remove regulatory arbitrage, empowering ESMA as the direct competent authority funded by industry fees. However, day-to-day enforcement may still be delegated to NCAs.

**What it means for firms:** The strategic advantage of locating in “friendly” jurisdictions (e.g., Cyprus or Malta) is effectively neutralised as EU standards become uniform. Firms will face increased costs due to direct supervisory fees and must adapt to a technocratic, data-heavy, English-language supervisory dialogue.

Next Steps: Firms must prepare for a transition to data-driven reporting rather than relationship management and budget for higher supervisory fees. Compliance frameworks must be reviewed to ensure they meet the rigorous “Single Rulebook” standard.

**Timeline:**

- **December 4, 2025:** Proposal adopted.

- Transfer Effective: 24 months after entry into force.
Impact Assessment:

- **Materiality:** High. “Forum shopping” strategies are obsolete; operations must be standardised across the EU.

### MiCA: ESMA statement on transitional measures under MiCA: December 2025

Jurisdiction/Regulator: European Union / ESMA

**Source:** [esma.europa.eu](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-12/ESMA75-113276571-1631_Statement_on_end_of_MiCA_transitional_periods.pdf)

**Summary of Development: The** ESMA issued a directive signalling the end of MiCA’s transitional periods and instructing NCAs to treat late applications with scepticism. It mandates “orderly wind-down plans” for unauthorised firms and explicitly rejects “reverse solicitation” as a sustainable strategy.

**What it means for firms:** Firms without authorisation or imminent approval face an existential “stop work” order. Lack of a wind-down plan is a regulatory breach, and reliance on reverse solicitation is now explicitly non-compliant.

**Next steps:** CROs must assess the authorisation status immediately. If approval is not guaranteed by December 30, wind-down plans must be put in place to avoid criminal charges or asset freezes.

**Timeline:**

- **December 4, 2025:** Directive issued.

- December 30, 2025: Grandfathering periods expire.
Impact Assessment:

- **Materiality:** Critical/Existential. Unauthorised firms face immediate closure.

**Summary of Development:** The ECB has moved the Digital Euro to an operational pilot focusing on “cash-like” offline functionality and privacy for low-value transactions. It utilises banks/PSPs for distribution and likely includes a ~€3,000 holding limit.

Private stablecoins may lose their “medium of exchange” role to state-backed alternatives and be forced to retreat to “store of value” or DeFi-specific use cases.

### DORA: Cyber Supply Chain Integrity: “Shai-Hulud 2.0”

Jurisdiction/Regulator: Luxembourg / CSSF (EU-wide impact)

**Source:** [cssf.lu](https://www.cssf.lu/en/2025/11/supply-chain-attack-using-npm-packages/)

**Summary of Development:** The CSSF warned of “Shai-Hulud 2.0,” a self-replicating worm targeting NPM packages in crypto tech stacks during the “preinstall” phase.3 The malware steals credentials and establishes persistence via self-hosted GitHub runners.4

**What it means for firms:** Under DORA, falling victim to this constitutes a failure of “ICT third-party risk” governance. Standard vulnerability scanners may miss this threat as it relies on behavioural execution.

**Next steps:** CISOs must conduct an immediate “Code Provenance Audit.” Pipelines must be hardened to restrict or monitor NPM pre-install scripts and implement behavioural analysis.

**Timeline:**

- November 28, 2025: Warning issued.
Impact Assessment:

- **Materiality:** Operational. A breach could lead to severe DORA penalties.

### MiCA: Technical Standards: The XBRL Mandate

Jurisdiction/Regulator: European Union / ESMA

**Source:** [esma.europa.eu](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-08/mica_taxonomy_2025_documentation_v1.0.pdf)

**Summary of Development: The**  ESMA has finalised standards requiring crypto-asset white papers to use Inline XBRs.5 This mandates that white papers be dynamic, machine-readable, and tagged data files rather than static PDFs.

**What it means for firms:** Issuers and trading platforms must abandon static document management in favour of regulatory reporting software capable of XBRL tagging. This aligns crypto issuance with public equity reporting standards.

**Next Steps:** Procure or license regulatory reporting software with XBRL capabilities. Convert current and drafting white papers into the specific MiCA taxonomy format.

**Timeline:**

- December 23, 2025: Obligations apply.
Impact Assessment:

- **Materiality:** Operational. Failure to comply halts issuance capabilities.

## United Kingdom

### Digital Markets: Draft Order creating new cryptoasset regulated activities laid before Parliament

**Source:** [legislation.gov.uk](http://legislation.gov.uk)

**Summary:** HM Treasury has laid the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2025 before Parliament.1 The Order amends the Regulated Activities Order (RAO) to define “qualifying cryptoassets,” designates activities such as operating a trading platform and issuing stablecoins as regulated, and introduces a market abuse framework and restrictions on public offers.2

**What it means for firms:** This is the primary legislation that transitions the UK from a registration-based (AML) regime to a comprehensive FSMA authorisation regime. It establishes the legal perimeter, meaning that most CASPs operating in or into the UK will need Part 4A authorisation by October 2027.

**Next Steps:** The Order must be approved by both Houses of Parliament (expected in 2026). Firms should prepare for the FCA application window.

**Timeline:** Parliamentary approval in 2026; Full regime commencement on 25 October 2027.

**Assessment:** Critical.

**Materiality:** Changes required. Unregulated firms must prepare for full authorisation or exit the UK market; public offer models (ICOs/IEOs) will be restricted to “qualifying” assets with compliant disclosures.

### Digital Markets: FCA consults on cryptoassets admissions and disclosures and market abuse regimes

**What it means for firms:** Exchanges effectively become “gatekeepers,” bearing liability for admitted assets if they cannot verify the issuer’s data. CASPs must implement sophisticated market surveillance systems to detect on-chain and off-chain abuse.12

**Timeline:** Consultation closes 12 February 2026; Regime applies 25 October 2027.

**Assessment:** High.

**Materiality:** Changes required. Listing committees must be overhauled to meet “Admissions” standards; “meme coin” listings may become unviable due to disclosure liabilities.

**What it means for firms:** CASPs must move from “capital light” models to holding regulatory capital proportionate to their activity levels (e.g., trading volume, assets under custody). An “Overall Risk Assessment” will replace the ICARA for these firms.

**Timeline:** Consultation closes 12 February 2026.

**Assessment:** High.

Business Model Risk: Changes required. Thinly capitalised firms may need to raise funds or exit; high-volume, low-margin businesses will face higher capital locks.

### FinCrime: FCA fines Nationwide Building Society for financial crime failings

Jurisdiction/Regulator: United Kingdom / FCA

**Source:** [fca.org.uk](https://www.fca.org.uk/news/press-releases/fca-fines-nationwide-44m-failings-financial-crime-controls)

**Summary:** The FCA fined Nationwide £44m for AML failings, specifically for ineffective systems in refreshing Customer Due Diligence (CDD) and failing to detect customers using personal accounts for business activity.3

**What it means for firms:** This enforcement action highlights a specific risk relevant to crypto: the use of personal accounts for business-like trading volumes (e.g., arbitrage or P2P merchants). The FCA expects firms to have ongoing monitoring capable of flagging when a “retail” user behaves like a commercial entity.

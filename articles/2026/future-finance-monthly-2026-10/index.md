## Executive Summary

This month, the UK opened its authorisation gateway and the OCC finalised its GENIUS Act rule for stablecoin issuers. ESMA began a three-month remediation period for non-MiCA stablecoins. The United States and the CFTC sued Utah over prediction markets, and Binance reported a USD 6.3 billion hot-wallet incident.

- **\[High\] European Union — non-MiCA stablecoins.** ESMA’s opinion of 8 October says CASPs should not provide services related to ARTs or EMTs that do not comply with MiCA. National authorities should require legacy exposures to be remediated within three months, which we compute as **8 January 2027.** Any continued service should be limited to sell-only, conversion, transfer or withdrawal. Press reports say one large exchange told EEA retail customers it would convert 14 assets that do not meet MiCA, including USDT, on or after 30 October.

- **\[High\] United Kingdom — the gateway is open.** The FCA opened applications on 30 September. Firms intending to continue operating should apply by **28 February 2027**, ahead of commencement on 25 October 2027. Consultation on draft perimeter and DeFi guidance (21 October) closes on **18 November.** Final guidance may not arrive until January or spring 2027, and the amending Regulations had not been made by 31 October.

- **\[High\] Asia-Pacific — Australia.** ASIC’s CP 392 (15 October) proposes standards for the platform licensing regime. Under ASIC’s proposed guidance, firms will need a licence or variation. The laws commence on **8 April 2027**, and existing operators must apply by 7 October 2027. Under two of its three proposed capital models, an operator without a qualifying third-party custodian would need net tangible assets of A\$7–14 million or more. Submissions close on **27 November**.

- **\[Medium\] Middle East — Binance incident; reserve and DeFi standards.** Binance reported hot wallets were compromised on 29 October (UTC), affecting about USD 6.3 billion. The ADGM FSRA said on 30 October it is monitoring and has set no requirements. Binance says it will absorb the loss, has replenished up to 90% of the stolen assets by 31 October, and expects withdrawals to reopen on 1 November. VARA set a minimum scope for reserve audits (6 October) and good practice for public disclosures (28 October). ADGM consults on DeFi guidance to **30 November**.

- **\[High\] United States and UK — sanctions and financial crime.** OFAC designated the A7 Network on 1 October, and FinCEN proposed a ban on certain transmittals of funds involving the A7 Network’s sub-agents (comments due **4 November**). On 8 October, the UK designated three crypto exchanges and two payment platforms, two linked to the A7 Network. Most designations publish no wallet addresses; in our view, screening must rely largely on names, aliases and attribution.

- **\[High\] United States — prediction markets.** Enacted on 19 October, the CFTC’s interim regulation preserves the exclusion for state-authorised gambling from swap status. In contrast, its draft rulemaking seeks to incorporate event derivatives within the scope (feedback due **18 November**). Following regulatory pushback from Ohio and Utah, federal authorities alongside the CFTC initiated litigation against Utah on 26 October, while pursuing actions in nine additional state jurisdictions. The agency also sanctioned a key performance indicator derivative for trading and clearing on 27 October.

- **\[High\] United States — GENIUS Act.** The OCC’s final rule (21 October) takes effect with the Act on **18 January 2027**. It drops the proposed rebuttable presumption on yield, and the OCC may grant pending applicants waivers of up to 12 months. Comments on the Federal Reserve’s two proposals and Treasury’s interim final rule close on **30 November**.

- **\[Medium\] International standard setters.** FinCEN reported on 30 October that the FATF had removed Bulgaria, Côte d’Ivoire, the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Monaco from its grey list, with the call-for-action list unchanged. The FATF’s own outcomes pages could not be read.

**Medium, in brief.** ESMA, the ESRB and AMLA outlined MiCA review priorities, including a DeFi-access service and expanding the scope to staking and lending. The EBA classified USDC as the first significant e-money token and began supervising its reserves on 12 November. AMLA submitted customer due diligence standards to the Commission. The Central Bank of Ireland fined Coinbase Ireland €3,252,618 for e-money safeguarding failures, pending High Court confirmation. The FCA consulted on crypto examples for its Financial Crime Guide and settled with HTX, which OFSI considers subject to UK sanctions. The UK Budget announced that HMRC’s Financial Institution Notice will apply to CASPs from Royal Assent to the Finance Bill 2026-27. MAS proposed FIDReC membership for payment institutions. Korea issued warnings on offshore perpetual futures, and Taiwan extended its Travel Rule to all transfers. FinCEN withdrew its unhosted-wallet and mixer proposals. The CFTC proposed a federal retail crypto regime (comments due 14 December), the SEC proposed a custody route for advisers (comments due 7 December), and Canada proposed stablecoin regulations (comments due 23 December).

## European Union

### 1. EU — non-MiCA stablecoins: ESMA starts a three-month remediation clock

> **MiCA:** ESMA sets out supervisory expectations on services related to unauthorised stablecoins; Opinion on the provision of crypto asset services in relation to non-MiCA-compliant asset-referenced tokens and e-money tokens

- **Jurisdiction/Regulator:** EU — European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA)

- **Publication Date:** 8 October 2026 (news item; opinion ESMA75-113276571-1742) (weekly edition of 10 October)

- **Source Link:** [ESMA news item](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-sets-out-supervisory-expectations-services-related-unauthorised) · [ESMA opinion](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-10/ESMA75-113276571-1742_Opinion_on_the_provision_of_crypto_asset_services_in_relation_to_non-MiCA-compliant_asset-referenced_tokens_and_e-money_tokens.pdf)

**Summary:** The opinion is addressed to national competent authorities under Article 29(1)(a) of the ESMA Regulation. It sets supervisory expectations; it is not a Level 1 or Level 2 measure.

- **The expectation.** “ESMA considers that CASPs should not provide crypto-asset services in relation to ARTs or EMTs that do not comply with MiCA (paragraph 12).

- **Scope.** The opinion applies to every CASP service under MiCA, individually or in combination, from operating a trading platform to custody and portfolio management (paragraph 21). Its test (footnote 1) is whether the conditions for a lawful offer to the public or admission to trading under Title III or Title IV of MiCA are met.

- **Legacy exposures.** National authorities should require remediation “no later than three (3) months following the date of publication of this Opinion” (paragraph 27). On our computation, that is **8 January 2027**.

- **What may continue.** Any continued service “should be strictly limited to sell-only, conversion, transfer or withdrawal functionalities” (paragraph 27).

**Market effect (secondary sources).** Press reports say Coinbase told EEA stablecoin, retail customers of Coinbase Luxembourg S.A. that, on or after 30 October, it would convert, on a one-time **basis,** 14 assets that do not meet MiCA, including USDT, into USDC or another supported stablecoin. Coinbase’s own notice was not read.

**What it means for firms:** The opinion reaches trading services (spot, OTC and one-click convert pairs quoted in a non-compliant stablecoin), yield, earn and staking products (stablecoin savings and loans), institutional services, and fiat and payment services (stablecoin settlement legs). Tokens that are not ARTs or EMTs fall outside it. The opinion names no token. It does not say how multi-issued or third-country-issued stablecoins are treated, and that will determine how many pairs are affected. In our view, the conversion reported in the window shows a large exchange applying the expectation well ahead of the remediation deadline. Read with entry 3: EU stablecoin policy is moving from listing decisions towards issuer supervision.

**Timeline:**

- 8 October 2026 — opinion published.

- 30 October 2026 — reported start of one exchange’s conversion for EEA customers (secondary sources).

- 8 January 2027 — end of the three-month remediation period (our computation from paragraph 27).

- Date each national authority applies the opinion — Not specified in source.

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality: High.** A supervisory expectation with a fixed clock. In our view, it targets pairs and products that may carry much of the EU stablecoin volume (share not determinable from the sources).

*Question for the next edition:* has any national authority said how it will apply the three-month period, and how it will treat multi-issued tokens?

### 2. EU — MiCA review: ESMA, the ESRB and AMLA say what should change

> **MiCA:** ESMA calls for changes to make MiCA clearer, safer and ready for emerging services; ESRB response to the European Commission’s targeted consultation on the review of Regulation on the Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA); AMLA identifies AML/CFT priorities for the review of MiCA

- **Jurisdiction/Regulator:** EU — ESMA; European Systemic Risk Board (ESRB); Anti-Money Laundering Authority (AMLA)

- **Publication Date:** 29 September 2026 (ESRB response); 30 September 2026 (ESMA); 7 October 2026 (AMLA) (weekly editions of 3 and 10 October)

- **Source Link:** [ESMA news item](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-calls-changes-make-mica-clearer-safer-and-ready-emerging-services) · [ESRB response](https://www.esrb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/other/ESRB.response260929~dc708d0e30.en.pdf) · [AMLA news item](https://www.amla.europa.eu/amla-identifies-amlcft-priorities-review-mica_en)

**Summary:** The Commission’s targeted and public consultations closed on 30 September. Three more EU bodies responded around the close, after the EBA and the ESCB (September edition, entry 1).

- **ESMA (30 September).** It recommends a new regulated crypto-asset service for firms that provide access to DeFi protocols. It also recommends proportionate requirements for staking, lending and borrowing, including through disclosure, and stricter marketing rules, particularly for influencers. Further proposals cover binding ESMA opinions on token classification, clearer criteria for genuinely decentralised activity, stronger powers over third-country firms that solicit EU investors, and powers to block fraudulent websites. ESMA also wants explicit rules to stop regulated firms from offering services linked to stablecoins that do not comply with MiCA, and more consistent prudential requirements.

- **ESRB (29 September).** “No, MiCA should disallow the multi-issuance model for stablecoins.” It also asks the Commission to set up, by the end of 2026, a framework to assess whether third-country stablecoin regimes align with MiCA, and to conduct liquidity and capital stress testing of EU issuers in multi-issuer schemes.

- **AMLA (7 October).** On staking, lending and borrowing: “AMLA suggests the Commission consider bringing these activities within the regulatory framework”. It also invites a legal definition and common criteria for who exercises effective control in DeFi, and a consistent EU-wide approach to unauthorised stablecoins. The page does not mention the Travel Rule.

**What it means for firms:** These are recommendations; the Commission and the co-legislators decide. The EBA, ESCB, and ESMA support some regulation of crypto lending, and AMLA asks the Commission to consider it; ESCB, ESMA, and AMLA also cover staking. If a DeFi-access service were created, Web3 wallets, DEX portals and Web3 earn (Web3, DeFi and wallet services) would move inside the perimeter. If multi-issuance were disallowed, we believe a stablecoin issued jointly by an EU and a third-country entity would need restructuring before it could be offered in the EU. If the recommendations are adopted, the board's structural question is whether the EU product plan for Earn and Web3 assumes these activities become authorised services.

**Timeline:**

- 29 September 2026 — ESRB response; 30 September 2026 — ESMA response; Commission consultations close.

- 7 October 2026 — AMLA response.

- 30 June 2027 — Commission report on the application of MiCA due, accompanied where appropriate by a legislative proposal (MiCA Article 140).

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality: Medium**, contingent. Direction of travel, structural if adopted.

### 3. EU — USDC becomes the first significant e-money token
> **MiCA:** The EBA classifies USDC as the first significant e-money token in the European Union

- **Jurisdiction/Regulator:** EU — European Banking Authority (EBA); Autorité de contrôle prudentiel et de résolution (ACPR)

- **Publication Date:** 14 October 2026 (weekly edition of 17 October)

- **Source Link:** [EBA press release](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/eba-classifies-usdc-first-significant-e-money-token-european-union)

**Summary:**

- The EBA classified USDC, issued by Circle Internet Financial Europe SAS, as a significant e-money token under MiCA. The release says 45 EMTs have been notified under MiCA; USDC is the first classified as significant.

- From **12 November 2026**, the EBA will supervise own funds, the reserve of assets, liquidity requirements, and recovery and redemption plans, moving these from the ACPR. The ACPR keeps areas such as governance, qualifying holdings, fit-and-proper assessment, outsourcing, and conflicts of interest.

- The EBA has signed a memorandum of understanding with the New York State Department of Financial Services on cross-border stablecoin activities. It reassesses significance annually.

**What it means for firms:** The release imposes nothing on CASPs. The lines that touch USDC are trading services (spot, convert and OTC pairs), fiat and payment services (stablecoin rails and remittance) and yield, earn and staking products (stablecoin yield). In our view, the change belongs in the token’s counterparty file: supervision of the features that drive redemption risk moves from a national authority to an EU one. Read with entry 1.

**Timeline:**

- 14 October 2026 — classification published.

- 12 November 2026 — EBA supervision of own funds, reserves, liquidity and redemption matters begins.

- Next annual significance assessment — Not specified in source.

**Assessment:**

- **Materiality: Medium.** No change to the CASP’s own permissions on the face of the release.

### 4. EU — AMLA’s customer due diligence rulebook goes to the Commission
> **FinCrime:** Press Release: AMLA finalises key standards for the private sector; Press Release: AMLA finalises standards on Home-Host Supervisory Cooperation; Press Release: AMLA consults on draft standards for an EU-wide AML/CFT database

- **Jurisdiction/Regulator:** EU — Anti-Money Laundering Authority (AMLA)

- **Publication Date:** 1 October 2026, with related releases on 2 and 7 October (weekly editions of 3 and 10 October)

- **Source Link:** [AMLA press release, 1 October](https://www.amla.europa.eu/press-release-amla-finalises-key-standards-private-sector_en) · [AMLA roundtables, 2 October](https://www.amla.europa.eu/amla-invites-stakeholders-sectoral-roundtables-simplified-customer-due-diligence_en) · [AMLA database consultation, 7 October](https://www.amla.europa.eu/press-release-amla-consults-draft-standards-eu-wide-amlcft-database_en)

**Summary:**

- **Final draft RTS (1 October).** AMLA submitted a draft RTS to the Commission under the AML Regulation on customer due diligence (Article 28(1)), business relationships and occasional or linked transactions (Article 19(9)), and group-wide arrangements (Articles 16(4) and 17(3)). “Once adopted and published in the Official Journal of the EU, they are proposed to apply six months after they enter into force.” **The release does not mention CASPs or the Travel Rule.**

- **Home-host cooperation (1 October).** Final draft RTS on supervisory cooperation between home and host authorities; CASPs are not named.

- **Simplified due diligence (2 October).** Sectoral roundtables to inform draft guidelines, to be held 9 November – 2 December in Frankfurt.

- **Central database (7 October).** Consultation, by public hearing, on draft RTS for a central AML/CFT database. The hearing runs 14:00–16:00 CET on **3 November 2026**, which is also the deadline (AMLA consultations page). The release does not mention crypto.

**What it means for firms:** CASPs are obliged entities under the AML Regulation, so that these standards will set onboarding, ongoing monitoring, and linked-transaction rules across trading, fiat ramps, P2P, and cards, plus group-wide controls for multi-entity CASP groups. The AML Regulation applies from 10 July 2027. In our view, the six-month application window is the planning constraint: re-papering can start on the submitted text rather than wait for the Official Journal.

**Timeline:**

- 1 October 2026 — RTS submitted to the Commission.

- 3 November 2026, 14:00–16:00 CET — public hearing; central database consultation closes at 16:00 CET.

**Summary:** Six tracks moved in one month.

**Assessment:**

- **25 October 2027** — UK: cryptoasset regime commences.

- **18 July 2028** — US: GENIUS Act offer prohibition on digital asset service providers (GENIUS Act section 3; Treasury proposal on implementation).

- **17 September 2031** — US: SEC Innovation Exemption relief ends.
